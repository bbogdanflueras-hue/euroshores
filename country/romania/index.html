Beach by beach in Romania

Gura Portitei Beach (4.4 / 5)

Gura Portitei is a narrow sand spit between the Black Sea and Lake Golovita, inside the Danube Delta Biosphere Reserve. Most visitors arrive by boat from Jurilovca, and the beach feels remote, with a small holiday village and wide, empty sand.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: A small holiday village with simple rooms and a restaurant; no resort facilities. Getting there and parking: Leave the car in Jurilovca and take the boat across the lake.

Corbu Beach (4.3 / 5)

Corbu is one of the last wild beaches on the Romanian coast, a long strip of fine sand north of Navodari with no hotels on the shore. It is reached by an unpaved road, and the space and quiet draw campers and people escaping the big resorts.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Very few facilities; a few seasonal bars near the main access point. Getting there and parking: Informal parking behind the beach at the end of a dirt road.

Vama Veche Beach (4.2 / 5)

Vama Veche is the last village before the Bulgarian border and has long had a reputation as the free-spirited, bohemian beach of Romania. The sand is mixed with shells, bars play live music into the night, and some visitors still camp near the shore.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars, sunbeds in some sections, small guesthouses in the village. Getting there and parking: Limited parking in the village; easiest by car or bus from Mangalia.

Sulina Beach (4.2 / 5)

Sulina is the easternmost town in Romania, where the Danube meets the Black Sea, and there is no road to it: you arrive by boat from Tulcea. Its beach is a long, wide stretch of fine sand a short way from town and is rarely crowded.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Basic facilities and a few bars near the main entrance. Getting there and parking: No road access: take the ferry or fast boat from Tulcea, then walk, cycle or take a local ride to the beach.

Mamaia Beach, Constanta (4.1 / 5)

Mamaia is the best-known resort in Romania, a strip of fine sand about 8 km long between the Black Sea and Lake Siutghiol, just north of Constanta. The water is shallow and calm on most days, and the shore is lined with hotels, sunbed concessions and bars.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas, beach bars, showers, lifeguards in season. Getting there and parking: Paid parking along the resort; buses from Constanta, and a seasonal cable car runs along part of the strip.

2 Mai Beach (4.1 / 5)

2 Mai is a small fishing village between Mangalia and Vama Veche, with a calmer, more family-friendly beach than its lively neighbour. Guesthouses take the place of big hotels, and part of the shore has long been used as a naturist area.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Some sunbeds and small beach bars, guesthouses in the village. Getting there and parking: Street parking in the village; buses and minibuses from Mangalia.

Neptun Beach (4.1 / 5)

Neptun is a green, low-rise resort north of Mangalia, set around small lakes and woodland, with a sandy beach that is usually calmer than Mamaia. Olimp and the Jupiter, Venus and Saturn resorts continue along the same stretch of coast.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas, bars, lifeguards in season. Getting there and parking: Parking in the resort; buses and trains between Constanta and Mangalia.

Costinesti Beach (4.0 / 5)

Costinesti is a lively resort popular with students and young travellers, with a sandy beach backed by low cliffs. North of the resort, the rusting wreck of the cargo ship Evangelia sits just offshore and is one of the best-known sights on the coast.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, bars and clubs, lifeguards in season. Getting there and parking: Parking in the resort; trains on the Constanta to Mangalia line stop at Costinesti.

Eforie Nord Beach (4.0 / 5)

Eforie Nord is a long-established spa resort just south of Constanta, between the sea and Lake Techirghiol, whose mineral mud is used in local treatments. The beach is wide and sandy, with calm, shallow water that suits families.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas, bars, lifeguards in season; spa centres nearby. Getting there and parking: Paid parking in town; trains and buses from Constanta.

About beaches in Romania

Romania has about 245 km of Black Sea coast, from the Danube Delta in the north to the Bulgarian border in the south. The southern half is lined with sandy resorts such as Mamaia, Eforie and Neptun, while the north, inside the Danube Delta Biosphere Reserve, has wild beaches like Sulina and Gura Portitei that you reach by boat.

When to go

The season runs from June to early September, and July and August are the warmest and busiest months, especially at weekends. The Black Sea is usually warm enough for swimming from mid-June, and early September is calmer, with warm water and fewer people.

Getting around

Constanta is the gateway to the coast, with an airport nearby and trains from Bucharest. Trains and buses link the southern resorts as far as Mangalia, and a car makes it easier to reach Corbu or Vama Veche. Sulina has no road and is reached by boat from Tulcea, and Gura Portitei by boat from Jurilovca.

Good to know

The currency is the Romanian leu, and cards are widely accepted in the resorts. Many beaches have paid sunbed sections next to free public sand. The Danube Delta beaches are inside a protected reserve, so follow local rules on camping, fires and wildlife.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to visit Romanian beaches?

July and August are warmest but busiest. Late June and early September give warm water with far fewer people.

Which Romanian beach is the quietest?

Corbu, Gura Portitei and Sulina are the wildest, with few or no hotels. The southern resorts such as Mamaia are much busier.
