Beach by beach in Bulgaria

Sunny Beach, Nessebar (4.0 / 5)

Sunny Beach is the largest resort on the Bulgarian Black Sea, with about eight kilometres of wide golden sand and shallow water. It is lively, with beach bars, water sports and sunbeds along the main stretch, and quieter corners at the far northern and southern ends.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas for hire, beach bars, water sports, showers and toilets. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks and street parking in the resort, busy in July and August.

Albena Beach, Balchik (4.0 / 5)

Albena is a purpose-built resort north of Varna with a broad, gently sloping sandy beach backed by dunes and woodland. The water is shallow and the beach is well kept, which makes it popular with families.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas for hire, beach bars, showers, toilets and water sports. Getting there and parking: Paid parking in the resort and near the beach.

Kiten Beach, Kiten (4.0 / 5)

Kiten is a small resort on the southern coast with fine sand and calmer surroundings than the big northern resorts. Its beaches are backed by a mix of small hotels and campsites.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas for hire in season, small bars and restaurants nearby. Getting there and parking: Street parking and small car parks in the village.

Irakli Beach, Obzor (4.0 / 5)

Irakli is a long, undeveloped sandy beach south of Obzor, backed by the forested hills of the Emine cape. It has no hotels, so it feels wild. Most visitors arrive on a path from the road, and part of the beach is known as an informal naturist spot.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Few facilities, a beach bar and basic services in high season. Getting there and parking: Roadside parking at the top, then a walk down to the beach.

Veleka Beach, Sinemorets (4.0 / 5)

Near Sinemorets, the Veleka river reaches the Black Sea inside the Strandzha Nature Park. The beach is a long strip of sand with the river on one side and forested hills behind, and it feels wilder than the northern resorts.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Few facilities, a small bar and seasonal services near the village. Getting there and parking: Parking near Sinemorets, then a short walk to the beach.

Obzor Beach, Obzor (4.0 / 5)

Obzor has a long sandy beach with a relaxed resort feel, a seafront promenade, restaurants and low-rise hotels. It is quieter than Sunny Beach, and the water is shallow near the shore.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas for hire, beach bars, showers and toilets. Getting there and parking: Street parking and car parks near the beach.

Arkutino Beach, Primorsko (4.0 / 5)

Arkutino is a sandy beach between Sozopol and Primorsko, set next to Arkutino lake and the Ropotamo nature reserve. It is calmer than the resort strips, with dunes and pines behind the sand.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas in season, a beach bar. Getting there and parking: Parking near the entrance and by the road.

Krapets Beach, Shabla (4.0 / 5)

Krapets is a small village on the northern coast near Shabla with a long sandy beach and few visitors. It is simple and low-key, with calm water in summer and a very different feel from the big resorts.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Few facilities, a couple of beach bars and guesthouses in the village. Getting there and parking: Parking in the village and near the beach.

Kavatsite Beach, Sozopol (4.0 / 5)

Kavatsite is a long sandy beach north of Sozopol, backed by dunes and a pine camping area. The water is shallow and calm, and the atmosphere is relaxed and informal.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas in season, beach bars, camping. Getting there and parking: Free and paid parking behind the beach.

Silistar Beach, Tsarevo (4.0 / 5)

Silistar is a quiet sandy beach on the far southern coast, close to the Strandzha Nature Park and the Turkish border region. It has a simple, uncrowded feel, with forested hills behind and few buildings nearby.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Few facilities, a seasonal bar or two and some camping nearby. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach access, then a short walk.

Vlas Beach, Sveti Vlas (4.0 / 5)

Sveti Vlas is a resort town at the northern end of Sunny Beach bay, with a sandy beach edged by rocks and dune grass. It is calmer than Sunny Beach and sits below the green slopes of the Balkan foothills.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas for hire, beach bars, showers and a marina nearby. Getting there and parking: Paid parking in the town and near the beach.

Chernomorets Beach, Sozopol (4.0 / 5)

Chernomorets is a village beach north of Sozopol with a long curve of sand and rows of sunbeds below a low headland. It is quieter than the big resorts and suits families who want shallow water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas for hire, beach bars, cafes and showers. Getting there and parking: Street parking and small car parks in the village.

Byala Beach, Byala (4.0 / 5)

Byala is a small town on the northern Black Sea coast with a sandy beach, a wooden pier and calm water. It is low-key, with a harbour feel and fewer large hotels than the main resorts.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas for hire in season, beach bars and cafes. Getting there and parking: Street parking in the town and near the pier.

Varna City Beach, Varna (4.0 / 5)

Varna city beach is a long strip of sand below the Sea Garden, within walking distance of the centre. It is easy to reach and lively in summer, and the water can be wavy when the wind blows from the east.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas for hire, showers, toilets, cafes and a promenade. Getting there and parking: Paid parking near the Sea Garden and on nearby streets.

Saints Constantine and Helena Beach, Varna (4.0 / 5)

Saints Constantine and Helena is the oldest seaside resort in Bulgaria, set in a park north of Varna. The beaches are small and sandy and sit below hotels and gardens, with a relaxed spa-resort feel.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas for hire, cafes, showers and spa hotels nearby. Getting there and parking: Paid parking in the resort and near the beaches.

Pomorie Beach, Pomorie (4.0 / 5)

Pomorie sits on a narrow peninsula, and its sandy beaches are lined with groynes and backed by apartments and promenades. The town is known for its salt lake and mineral spa, and it is quieter than Sunny Beach.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas for hire, beach bars, cafes and showers. Getting there and parking: Street parking in the town and small car parks near the beach.

Primorsko Beach, Primorsko (4.0 / 5)

Primorsko is a resort town on the southern Black Sea coast with long sandy beaches, shallow water and a seafront promenade. It is popular with families and young people and has forested hills behind.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas for hire, beach bars, showers and water sports. Getting there and parking: Street parking and car parks near the seafront.

Golden Sands Beach, Varna (4.0 / 5)

Golden Sands is one of the largest Bulgarian resorts, set inside a nature park north of Varna. The beach is a wide band of fine sand with shallow water, and there are many bars, rides and water sports.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas for hire, beach bars, water sports, showers and toilets. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks in the resort.

Ravda Beach, Nessebar (4.0 / 5)

Ravda is a quieter resort village south of Nessebar with a long sandy beach and shallow water. It is more relaxed than Sunny Beach, with a mix of hotels, restaurants and local life.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas for hire, beach bars, showers and toilets. Getting there and parking: Street parking and car parks near the beach.

About beaches in Bulgaria

Bulgaria's Black Sea coast runs for about 380 kilometres from the Romanian border to Turkey, with long sandy beaches, low cliffs and some of the least developed shoreline in Europe. Big resorts such as Sunny Beach and Albena have wide golden sand and full facilities, while Irakli, Veleka and Krapets are quiet and wild.

When to go

June to September is the main swimming season, with the warmest sea in July and August. June and September are calmer, while the north coast can feel cooler and windier than the south.

Getting around

Most visitors fly into Varna or Burgas, and both airports are within an hour or two of the main resorts. Local buses and minibuses link the towns, and a hire car helps for the wild southern beaches near Sinemorets and Kiten.

Good to know

Sunbeds and umbrellas are usually paid at developed resort beaches, while the wilder southern beaches have few services, so bring water and shade. Check flags and local signs before swimming, since the Black Sea can have currents after strong winds.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to swim in Bulgaria

July and August have the warmest water and the busiest beaches. June and September are quieter, with warm days and cooler evenings.

Which Bulgarian beaches are best for a quiet day

Irakli, Veleka, Krapets, Arkutino and Kavatsite are quieter than the big resorts, although most need a short walk or a drive from the nearest village.
