Beach by beach in Turkey

Kaputas Beach, Kalkan (4.7 / 5)

Kaputas is a small sandy cove at the mouth of a narrow canyon on the coast road between Kalkan and Kas. It is reached by a long flight of steps down from the road, and the water is a vivid turquoise.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 26 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Sunbeds and a small cafe in season. Getting there and parking: Roadside parking along the main road above the beach.

Patara Beach, Kas (4.7 / 5)

Patara is one of the longest sandy beaches on the Turkish coast, with dunes behind it and the ruins of the ancient Lycian city of Patara close by. The sea is clear with waves at times, and the beach feels open and uncrowded outside peak season. Sea turtles nest here, so keep to marked areas.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 26 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and a beach cafe, few other facilities. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach entrance.

Oludeniz Beach, Fethiye (4.6 / 5)

Oludeniz is a famous turquoise lagoon and beach near Fethiye, backed by pine-covered mountains. The sheltered lagoon has calm, clear water, and the main beach is a mix of sand and pebbles. Paragliders from Babadag mountain land on the seafront, which adds to the scene.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 26 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas, cafes and restaurants, water sports. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks near the seafront, and dolmus minibuses from Fethiye.

Iztuzu Beach, Dalyan (4.6 / 5)

Iztuzu is a long, narrow sandy beach on a sandbar between the Mediterranean and the Dalyan river delta. It is a protected nesting site for loggerhead turtles, so rules apply to keep the sand and the night-time beach undisturbed. Boats run to the beach from Dalyan.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 26 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas, cafes, a turtle information area. Getting there and parking: Car park at the beach, and boat trips from Dalyan.

Cirali Beach, Kemer (4.5 / 5)

Cirali is a long pebble beach in a small protected village near the ancient city of Olympos. Pensions and simple restaurants sit behind the beach instead of large hotels, and the Chimaera flames burn on the hillside a short walk away. Sea turtles nest on this coast.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 26 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Pensions with restaurants, simple sunbeds. Getting there and parking: Parking at the pensions and near the village entrance.

Butterfly Valley Beach, Faralya (4.5 / 5)

Butterfly Valley is a hidden cove at the foot of steep cliffs and a forested canyon near Faralya. Most visitors arrive by boat from Oludeniz, and there is no road to the beach. The setting is quiet and dramatic, with a small waterfall inside the valley.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 26 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: A simple cafe and basic camp facilities. Getting there and parking: No road access, so take a boat from Oludeniz.

Kabak Bay, Fethiye (4.5 / 5)

Kabak is a quiet bay at the bottom of a forested valley near Faralya, on the Lycian Way walking route. The beach is reached by a steep road and paths, and small camps and cafes serve the few visitors. It feels far from the resort coast.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 26 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: Camps with simple restaurants, basic facilities. Getting there and parking: Parking at the top of the valley, with a walk or shuttle down to the beach.

Cleopatra Beach, Alanya (4.4 / 5)

Cleopatra Beach is a long stretch of fine golden sand on the west side of the castle hill in Alanya. Legend says Cleopatra and Mark Antony swam here, which gave the beach its name. The sea is shallow and calm, and the town is a short walk away.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 26 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas, cafes, water sports. Getting there and parking: Parking in town and dolmus services from the centre.

Konyaalti Beach, Antalya (4.3 / 5)

Konyaalti is a long pebble beach on the western side of Antalya, with the Taurus Mountains rising behind the sea. A promenade runs along the shore, and the city, shops and restaurants are close by. The sea is clear and the beach is easy to reach.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 26 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas, showers, cafes, a promenade. Getting there and parking: Parking along the promenade, and the tram and buses from the centre.

Icmeler Beach, Marmaris (4.2 / 5)

Icmeler is a sandy bay beach in a resort village a short distance from Marmaris. The water is calm and shallow, and cafes, shops and restaurants line the seafront. It suits families who want a gentle swim and services close to the sand.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 26 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas, cafes, restaurants, water sports. Getting there and parking: Roadside and paid parking in the village, and boats or minibuses from Marmaris.

About beaches in Turkey

Turkey's Mediterranean and Aegean coasts have long sandy beaches, turquoise lagoons, pine-covered bays and some of the warmest sea in Europe. Famous names include Oludeniz, Patara and Iztuzu, while smaller coves around Kas, Kabak and Cirali are quieter. Most resort beaches have sunbeds and cafes in season.

When to go

May to October is the main swimming season, with the warmest sea in July, August and September. June and September give hot weather with fewer people, while July and August are the busiest and hottest months.

Getting around

The main gateways are Dalaman, Antalya, Bodrum and Izmir airports. Dolmus minibuses link coastal towns cheaply, and a hire car is the easiest way to reach remote bays. Some coves can only be reached by boat or on foot.

Good to know

Turkey uses the Turkish lira. Sunbeds and umbrellas are usually paid, and some beaches charge an entrance fee. Public nudity is not allowed, so bring swimwear, and check flags and local signs before swimming.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to swim in Turkey?

June and September offer hot weather and warm water with fewer people. July and August are the hottest and busiest months, and the sea stays warm into October.

Do you need a car to visit Turkey beaches?

Resort beaches such as Oludeniz, Konyaalti and Icmeler are easy to reach by bus or dolmus, but a car helps for quieter bays such as Kaputas, Kabak and Cirali.
