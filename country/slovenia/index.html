Beach by beach in Slovenia

Moon Bay (Mesecev Zaliv), Strunjan (4.4 / 5)

Moon Bay is a crescent-shaped pebble cove below the Strunjan cliff, the highest on the Slovenian coast, inside Strunjan Landscape Park. A steep path leads down from the cliff top, and there are no buildings on the bay.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: No facilities; the nearest bars are in Strunjan. Getting there and parking: Park in Strunjan or near the cliff-top road, then walk down a steep path.

Punta Beach, Piran (4.2 / 5)

Swimming in Piran means stepping off the stone and concrete platforms that run around the tip of the old town, below the lighthouse and the church of St Clement. The water is clear and deep close to shore, with the medieval town right behind you.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Ladders into the sea, cafes and ice cream nearby, no sand. Getting there and parking: Piran old town is car-free: park at the Fornace garage and take the shuttle bus into town.

Fiesa Beach (4.2 / 5)

Fiesa is a small pebble bay below green slopes between Piran and Strunjan, next to two small lakes that are a protected natural area. It is quieter than Piran and Portoroz, and you can walk there along the coast path from Piran.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: A hotel and campsite near the bay, a bar in season. Getting there and parking: Limited parking near the hotel; most people walk from Piran in about 20 minutes.

Debeli Rtic Beach (4.2 / 5)

Debeli rtic is a protected headland at the north-western tip of the Slovenian coast, near the Italian border, with low cliffs, woodland and small pebble coves. It is a natural monument and much quieter than the resorts to the south.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Few facilities on the coves; a health and holiday centre nearby. Getting there and parking: Parking near the Debeli rtic centre, then a short walk to the coves.

Simonov Zaliv Beach, Izola (4.1 / 5)

Simonov zaliv is a bay just west of Izola with a grassy lawn, a pebble and concrete shore and clear water, close to the remains of the Roman port of Haliaetum. It is one of the more relaxed organised beaches on the coast.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, showers, a bar and a hotel next to the beach. Getting there and parking: Parking near the hotel; Izola centre is a short walk away.

Portoroz Central Beach (4.0 / 5)

Portoroz has the largest organised beach in Slovenia, a sand-covered stretch in front of the resort hotels with shallow, calm water. It is lively and well equipped, and suits families with small children.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas, showers, changing rooms, lifeguards and bars in season. Getting there and parking: Paid parking in Portoroz; buses from Koper and Piran.

About beaches in Slovenia

Slovenia has under 50 km of Adriatic coast, between Italy and Croatia, so its beaches are small and close together. Swimming here mostly means stone or concrete platforms in towns such as Piran and Izola, a sandy resort beach at Portoroz, and quieter pebble coves under the cliffs of Strunjan and Debeli rtic.

When to go

The swimming season runs from June to September, with the warmest water in July and August. June and September are quieter and still warm, and the coast is an easy day trip from Ljubljana or Trieste at any point in summer.

Getting around

Koper, Izola, Piran and Portoroz are linked by frequent buses, and the coast is about 90 minutes by car from Ljubljana. Piran old town is closed to cars, so visitors park at the edge of town and take a shuttle bus in. Coastal paths connect several beaches on foot.

Good to know

Slovenia uses the euro. Much of the shore is rock or concrete rather than sand, so water shoes are useful. Strunjan and Debeli rtic are protected areas, so follow the rules on access, camping and fires.

Frequently asked questions

Does Slovenia have sandy beaches?

Very few. Portoroz has the main sandy beach; most other spots are pebble coves or stone and concrete platforms.

What is the best beach in Slovenia?

Moon Bay below the Strunjan cliff is the most scenic. For facilities and families, Portoroz is the easiest choice.
