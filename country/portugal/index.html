Beach by beach in Portugal

Benagil Cove (4.7 / 5)

A small cove leading to a sea cave with a skylight in its roof — best seen by kayak or paddleboard.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Kayak and boat tours from the beach. Getting there and parking: Village car park, short walk.

Praia da Falésia (4.7 / 5)

A long golden beach backed by red and ochre cliffs and pine trees, stretching toward Vilamoura.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 21 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Lifeguards in summer, beach bars, sunbed hire and showers at the main access points. Getting there and parking: Several car parks along the clifftop, then stairs down (about 80 steps at the main entrance).

Praia do Camilo (4.7 / 5)

A small golden cove framed by honey coloured cliffs and rock stacks near Lagos, reached by a long wooden staircase of around 200 steps.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: A restaurant and toilets at the top of the stairs, with little at sea level; lifeguards in season. Getting there and parking: A small car park by the stairs fills quickly in summer, so arrive early or walk from Lagos.

Praia da Ursa (4.7 / 5)

A small, wild sand cove below steep cliffs near Cabo da Roca, known for its tall rock stacks and rough Atlantic surf.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 17 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: None, with no lifeguards or services. Getting there and parking: Park near Azóia or Cabo da Roca and take the steep, unofficial cliff path down, which takes roughly 20 to 30 minutes and is tiring to climb back up.

Praia do Carvalho (4.7 / 5)

A small golden cove enclosed by tall Algarve cliffs, reached through a short tunnel cut into the rock and down a flight of steps.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 19 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Seasonal beach bar and sunbed hire in summer. Getting there and parking: Park in the small lot above the beach near Lagoa and Carvoeiro, then walk through the tunnel and down the stairs, which are steep on the way back up.

Praia da Bordeira (4.7 / 5)

A wide, wild Atlantic beach on the west Algarve coast with dunes, cliffs and a shallow lagoon that forms behind the sand. A quieter alternative to the busy south-coast beaches.

Best season: May–September. Typical water temperature: about 21 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Basic facilities in season; check locally. Getting there and parking: Car park near the beach; check locally.

Praia da Marinha (4.6 / 5)

Dramatic golden limestone arches frame this hidden gem on the Algarve coast — one of the most beautiful beaches in all of Portugal.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Basic facilities in season, cliff-top viewpoint. Getting there and parking: Car park at the top of the cliff stairs.

Praia Dona Ana (4.6 / 5)

Golden cliffs, sea stacks and a sheltered turquoise cove, a short walk from Lagos town.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 21 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Steps down to the beach; restaurants and shops in nearby Lagos. Getting there and parking: Car park near the top of the steps (fills early in summer).

Praia de Odeceixe (4.6 / 5)

A horseshoe shaped beach on the west coast of the Algarve where the Ribeira de Seixe river curls behind the sand to meet the Atlantic, with calm river pools on one side and surf on the other.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Toilets, showers, cafes and a surf school in season, with lifeguards in summer. Getting there and parking: By car to the village and beach car parks, with a short walk to the sand.

Praia da Comporta (4.6 / 5)

A long, quiet stretch of pale sand backed by dunes and pine woods on the Alentejo coast.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach restaurants and sunbeds at some access points. Getting there and parking: Parking at the main access points, busy in July and August.

Praia do Amado (4.6 / 5)

A broad sandy beach with hills behind it on the west coast of the Algarve, popular with surfers.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 19 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Surf school and beach cafe in season. Getting there and parking: Car park above the beach.

Praia do Beliche (4.6 / 5)

A sheltered sandy cove near Sagres on the far south-west tip of Portugal, backed by tall cliffs and facing the open Atlantic with golden sand and clear water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Limited facilities; check locally. Getting there and parking: Small car park above the beach; check locally.

Praia da Albandeira (4.6 / 5)

A small, sheltered cove at the foot of golden limestone cliffs in the Algarve, with a natural rock arch and clear water. Reached by a short path and steps from the clifftop.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: A seasonal snack bar at the top of the steps; check locally. Getting there and parking: Free car park at the clifftop; check locally.

Ilha de Tavira (4.6 / 5)

A long barrier island off the eastern Algarve with wide sandy beaches, calm lagoon water on one side and the open Atlantic on the other. Reached by a short ferry or boat from Tavira.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Cafes and restaurants near the ferry landing; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking is on the mainland near the ferry; check locally.

Praia da Adraga (4.5 / 5)

A golden Atlantic beach below eroded cliffs and rock arches on the Sintra coast, with strong surf and a beach restaurant.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 17 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach restaurant, with lifeguards in summer. Getting there and parking: A small car park sits above the beach, with a short walk down to the sand. It fills quickly on summer weekends, so arrive early.

Praia da Luz (4.5 / 5)

A sandy beach beside a small Algarve village near Lagos, with rocky outcrops and a seafront walk.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes, restaurants and sunbed rental nearby. Getting there and parking: Parking in the village and near the seafront; limited in summer.

Meia Praia (4.5 / 5)

A very long sandy beach east of Lagos, backed by dunes and reached by wooden walkways.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars and sunbed rental in sections. Getting there and parking: Parking at several access points along the beach.

Praia do Vale Figueiras (4.5 / 5)

A quiet Atlantic beach on the west Algarve coast with golden sand, low cliffs and room to spread out away from the busier south-coast resorts.

Best season: May–September. Typical water temperature: about 21 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Basic facilities in season; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking nearby; check locally.

Praia da Coelha (4.5 / 5)

A small sandy beach on the Algarve coast near Albufeira, set below golden cliffs with clear water and a quieter feel than the main resort beaches.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Basic facilities in season; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking nearby; check locally.

Praia do Barril (4.5 / 5)

A wide sandy beach on the barrier island of the Ria Formosa, reached from Santa Luzia by a flat footpath or a short tourist train. Rows of rusting anchors in the dunes behind the sand are a memorial to the local tuna fishing community.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Bars, restaurants and toilets in season, with a playground at peak times. Getting there and parking: Paid car park near the start of the path in Santa Luzia.

Praia do Guincho (4.4 / 5)

A long, wind-blown Atlantic beach west of Cascais, backed by dunes and the Sintra hills and known as one of the best windsurfing and kitesurfing spots in Europe.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lifeguard in the bathing season, restaurants nearby and surf and kite schools. Getting there and parking: A large car park at the north end is paid in summer, while free parking at the south end needs a short walk. Local bus 15 runs from Cascais.

Praia da Arrifana (4.4 / 5)

A crescent bay below steep cliffs on the west coast of the Algarve, with fishermen houses at the foot of a winding road and consistent waves that suit surfers.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Toilets and showers at the top car park, nearby restaurants and cafes, and lifeguards in the bathing season. Getting there and parking: A car park at the top of the cliff, then a zig-zag road down to the beach. It fills quickly on busy days.

Praia do Tonel (4.4 / 5)

A sandy Atlantic beach below the town of Sagres, open to the ocean and popular with surfers.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach cafe and surf schools nearby in season. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach; check locally for fees.

Praia da Rocha (4.4 / 5)

A very wide sand beach below golden Algarve cliffs and rock formations, with a long promenade behind.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, umbrellas, restaurants and bars; lifeguards in season. Getting there and parking: Car parks and street parking near the promenade; busy in summer.

Praia de Porto de Mos (4.4 / 5)

A broad sandy beach below golden cliffs a few kilometres west of Lagos. The Atlantic water is cooler than in the Mediterranean.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 19 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Seasonal beach bar and sunbed rental (fees unconfirmed). Getting there and parking: Car park above the beach; fee unconfirmed.

Praia de Santa Cruz (4.4 / 5)

A wide Atlantic beach of golden sand with dramatic rock stacks, popular with surfers and known for its golden evening light.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 19 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes, a surf school and sunbeds in season; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach; check locally.

Praia do Seixal (4.4 / 5)

A small beach of dark volcanic sand on the north coast of Madeira, framed by steep green cliffs. A natural sea pool and a few cafes sit nearby.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 21 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes and a natural pool nearby; check locally. Getting there and parking: Small car park near the beach; check locally.

Praia de Burgau (4.4 / 5)

A small sandy beach in the western Algarve, backed by the whitewashed houses of Burgau village and framed by green hills. Swimming is usually calmer than on the open west coast.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 21 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes and restaurants in the village; check locally. Getting there and parking: Street parking in the village and a car park nearby; check locally.

Praia do Castelejo (4.4 / 5)

A wide Atlantic beach on the wild western Algarve, set below tilted dark schist cliffs and rolling hills. Waves are strong, the water is cold and the beach stays quiet even in summer, with surfers and walkers as the main visitors.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 19 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Small beach bar in season, seasonal lifeguard, no sunbed rental. Getting there and parking: Free dirt car park above the beach; short walk down.

Ribeira d'Ilhas (4.3 / 5)

A sandy cove with rocky edges and steady Atlantic surf, a short drive from the village of Ericeira.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Seasonal beach bar and surf schools nearby. Getting there and parking: Car park above the beach; steps lead down.

Praia de Alvor (4.3 / 5)

A long, wide sandy beach next to the Alvor estuary in the western Algarve, with space to spread out and calm conditions on most summer days.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach cafes and restaurants nearby in Alvor; check locally. Getting there and parking: Car parks near the beach and town; check locally.

Praia do Meco (4.3 / 5)

A long, open Atlantic beach on the coast south of Lisbon, backed by low cliffs, with golden sand, strong surf and a relaxed local feel.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach restaurants near the main access; check locally. Getting there and parking: Car parks near the main access; check locally.

Praia de Carcavelos (4.3 / 5)

A wide sandy beach on the Lisbon-Cascais coast, backed by a promenade. Surf schools run lessons here and the beach is reached by train from central Lisbon.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 19 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lifeguards in season, surf schools, toilets and showers, cafes along the promenade. Getting there and parking: Free and paid parking nearby, which fills quickly on summer weekends.

Praia de Monte Gordo (4.3 / 5)

A broad, gently sloping beach on the eastern Algarve with shallow water, long sand and a Blue Flag. A promenade runs behind it with restaurants and shops.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lifeguards in summer, sunbed rental, toilets and showers on the promenade, restaurants. Getting there and parking: Free and paid parking nearby; busy at peak times.

Nazare Beach (4.2 / 5)

A wide Atlantic beach beneath tall cliffs beside the fishing town of Nazare, known for strong waves and fresh seafood.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 19 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars, restaurants and sunbeds in season. Getting there and parking: Paid parking along the seafront.

Praia de Faro (4.0 / 5)

A long sandy strip on a barrier island beside the Ria Formosa lagoon, reached by a short bridge from Faro.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 21 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars, restaurants and sunbeds in season. Getting there and parking: Car park at the entrance to the island.

About beaches in Portugal

Portugal's best-known beaches sit on the Algarve coast, where golden limestone cliffs, sea arches and sea caves frame small sandy coves. The Atlantic is cooler than the Mediterranean, but the scenery is hard to beat.

When to go

June to September is the main season, and July and August are the busiest. The Atlantic usually peaks at around 20 to 22 degrees C, cooler than the Mediterranean, and the Algarve stays sunny well into October. Morning visits are calmer, especially for boat and kayak trips.

Getting around

Faro airport is the main gateway to the Algarve. A car makes it easy to reach clifftop car parks, and many beaches are reached by stairs down the cliff. Boat and kayak trips run from nearby towns to sea caves such as Benagil.

Good to know

Portugal uses the euro. Stairs to some beaches can be long and steep, and Atlantic swell and currents can be strong, so follow the flags and lifeguard advice on supervised beaches.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to visit the Algarve beaches?

June to September for warm weather, with early mornings or September for fewer people. The sea stays cooler than in the Mediterranean all year.

Can you visit the Benagil cave without a boat?

Many people reach it by boat, kayak or paddleboard from nearby beaches. Conditions and rules change, so check locally before you go.
