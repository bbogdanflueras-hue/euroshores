Beach by beach in Italy

Cala Goloritzé (4.8 / 5)

A secluded cove under a limestone needle, reached only on foot or by boat — worth every step.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: None — carry water and food. Getting there and parking: Su Porteddu car park (included in the ticket), then a steep hike.

Cala Mariolu (4.8 / 5)

A pebble cove with vivid turquoise water below steep cliffs, reachable only by boat or on foot.

Best season: May–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: A small seasonal kiosk — bring water and food. Getting there and parking: No road: boats run from Cala Gonone, Santa Maria Navarrese and Arbatax, or hike about three hours from the Golgo plateau near Baunei.

Spiaggia dei Conigli (4.8 / 5)

A sweep of pale sand and shallow turquoise water on Lampedusa, set inside a nature reserve and often counted among the most beautiful beaches in the Mediterranean.

Best season: June–October. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: None on the beach: no lifeguards, toilets, shade or refreshments, so bring water, food and sun protection. Getting there and parking: About an 800 metre walk from the road, with a bus, bike or scooter to the trailhead. In summer entry is limited to booked shifts through the reserve, so reserve ahead on the official booking site.

Cala Brandinchi (4.8 / 5)

A long bay of fine pale sand and very shallow, clear water backed by stone pines, with Tavolara island rising on the horizon.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Seasonal bar and sunbed hire in summer. Getting there and parking: Paid car parking near the entrance, with a short walk to the beach through the pines. In peak summer, entry and parking can be limited or need advance booking, so check the current rules.

Spiaggia Grande (4.7 / 5)

Nestled beneath the pastel-coloured cliffside village of Positano — one of the most dramatic and romantic settings in Europe.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbed rental, restaurants, showers. Getting there and parking: Limited — use public transport or village car parks.

La Pelosa (4.7 / 5)

A shallow white-sand lagoon facing the Torre della Pelosa at the northwest tip of Sardinia.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbed rental and a snack bar. Getting there and parking: Paid parking in Stintino, then a short walk.

Cala Violina (4.7 / 5)

A crescent of fine pale sand inside the Bandite di Scarlino nature reserve on the Tuscan coast, said to squeak underfoot, which is how it got its name.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: A bar and snack kiosk at the car park, with little at the beach itself and no bins, so carry your litter out. Getting there and parking: Car park at the end of a dirt track off the SS322, then a shaded walk of about 1.5 km through the reserve. From June to September entry is capped and booked online in advance, so check the Scarlino reserve website for current fees.

Cala Luna (4.7 / 5)

A crescent of pale pebbles and coarse sand between limestone cliffs, with caves along the back and a freshwater pond behind the beach.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: Seasonal snack bar in summer; otherwise bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: No road access. Take a boat from Cala Gonone, or walk the coastal trail from Cala Fuili, which is a long hike in both directions.

Tuerredda Beach (4.7 / 5)

A crescent of fine pale sand and shallow turquoise water on the south coast of Sardinia, facing a small island just offshore.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Seasonal kiosk and sunbed rental in summer. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach is usually paid in summer and fills early, so arrive in the morning.

Porto Giunco (4.7 / 5)

A wide sandy beach in the south-east of Sardinia, with an old watchtower on the headland and a lagoon behind. The water is clear and shades from pale to deep turquoise.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Limited facilities; check what is open in season. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach in season; fee unconfirmed.

Baia di Ieranto (4.7 / 5)

A secluded cove on the Sorrentine peninsula, set between steep rocky headlands with deep turquoise water and a rugged, unspoilt shoreline.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: No facilities expected; bring supplies. Getting there and parking: Parking in the village above; the cove is reached on foot.

La Cinta Beach (4.7 / 5)

A long strip of soft white sand and shallow turquoise water just outside San Teodoro, with a lagoon behind it. It is one of the most popular beaches in north-east Sardinia and a favourite for families.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach clubs with sunbeds, bars, restaurants, showers and toilets. Getting there and parking: Free and paid parking near the entrances; paid lots are pricey in summer.

Scala dei Turchi (4.6 / 5)

A dazzling white marl cliff carved into giant steps above a small sandy beach on the south coast of Sicily, near Agrigento.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Limited at the cliff itself, with cafes and a small shop in the area behind. Getting there and parking: By car to the car parks near Realmonte; the cliff is reached on foot. Access has been capped and ticketed in recent seasons, so check the current rules for Realmonte before you go.

Cala Fuili (4.6 / 5)

A pebble and rock cove near Cala Gonone on the east coast of Sardinia, set below green limestone cliffs.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: No facilities at the cove; services in Cala Gonone. Getting there and parking: Parking near the access path, then a short walk down.

Tropea Beach (4.6 / 5)

A sweep of pale sand and clear blue water below the cliff-top town of Tropea on the Calabrian coast.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Sunbeds and beach clubs in season. Getting there and parking: Paid parking in Tropea; limited in summer.

San Vito Lo Capo (4.6 / 5)

A broad curve of pale sand and clear shallow water at the tip of north-west Sicily, backed by the town and a rocky headland.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, umbrellas, bars and restaurants in season. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks in town; fills quickly in August.

Punta Molentis (4.6 / 5)

A small beach of pale sand and clear shallow water near Villasimius, between low granite rocks. It is popular in summer, so space is limited at peak times.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Limited facilities; sunbed fees unconfirmed. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach; fee unconfirmed.

Li Cossi (4.6 / 5)

A small sandy cove on the north coast of Sardinia near Costa Paradiso, set between red granite rocks with green-blue water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Limited facilities; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking above the cove; limited in peak season.

Spiaggia dei Gabbiani (4.6 / 5)

A pebble cove on the Conero Riviera in the Marche, set below pale cliffs with clear turquoise water along a quiet stretch of the Adriatic coast.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: Limited facilities; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking above the coast; the beach is reached on foot or by boat, check locally.

Cala degli Inglesi (4.6 / 5)

A rocky cove on San Domino in the Tremiti Islands, with clear blue water and a rugged shoreline.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: No facilities expected; check locally. Getting there and parking: Visitors arrive by ferry or boat; check local transport.

Piscina Rei Beach (4.6 / 5)

A long beach of fine sand on the south-east coast of Sardinia in the municipality of Muravera, with clear water and dunes behind the shore.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Limited facilities; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach; check locally.

Baia delle Zagare (4.6 / 5)

A pebble cove on the Gargano coast of Puglia, known for its white limestone sea stacks, clear turquoise water and dramatic cliffs backed by pine trees.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Limited facilities; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking is limited in peak season; check locally.

Lama Monachile (4.6 / 5)

A small pebble cove at the foot of Polignano a Mare, framed by tall limestone cliffs and houses built right at the edge. Clear, deep water close to shore and a short walk from the old town.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes and restaurants in the old town above; check locally. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks on the edge of town, then a short walk; check locally.

Spiaggia di Piscinas (4.6 / 5)

A wild, long beach backed by some of the tallest sand dunes in Europe, on the Costa Verde of south-west Sardinia. Open, quiet and a long way from the resort strips.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: A seasonal bar or snack stand near the beach; otherwise very few facilities. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach reached by a long unpaved road; check locally.

Cala Sisine (4.6 / 5)

A remote cove in the Gulf of Orosei, where a green canyon opens onto pale sand and shingle. It is reached by boat or on foot, which keeps it quieter than the big resort beaches.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: A small seasonal bar; otherwise no facilities, so bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: No parking at the beach; boats run from Cala Gonone and Santa Maria Navarrese, or you can walk from the Golgo plateau parking areas.

Calamosche Beach (4.6 / 5)

A small sandy cove between rocky headlands inside the Vendicari nature reserve on the south-east coast of Sicily. The beach is reached on foot from the car park and has no shade.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: No sunbeds and no shade; a bar and showers are reported at the car park. Getting there and parking: Private car park at the reserve, about 5 EUR reported, plus a reserve entry fee.

Torre Guaceto Beach (4.6 / 5)

A long, flat, sandy beach inside the Torre Guaceto marine reserve on the Adriatic coast of Puglia. Dunes, Mediterranean scrub and a 16th-century watchtower give it a wild feel, and the sea is shallow and clean.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Reserve visitor services, a small bar and toilets near the entrance, no sunbed rental on most of the beach. Getting there and parking: Paid parking at the reserve entrance (a few euros per day) with a shuttle to the beach, or a flat walk of about 1.4 km.

Torre dell'Orso Beach (4.6 / 5)

A wide crescent of pale sand and shallow turquoise water in Salento, guarded by a watchtower and the famous Two Sisters rock stacks. Clear water and gentle depths make it a favourite for families.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lidos with sunbeds and umbrellas, free beach areas, bars and restaurants. Getting there and parking: Paid and free car parks near the seafront; they fill quickly in summer.

Spiaggia di Portonovo (4.6 / 5)

Portonovo is a bay of white pebbles and clear blue water below the green cliffs of the Conero headland. A small medieval church and a Napoleonic fort stand behind the beach, and the setting is one of the finest on the Adriatic coast.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars, sunbeds in season, a few restaurants and showers. Getting there and parking: Paid parking at the top of the access road; shuttle bus runs down in season.

Baia del Silenzio (4.5 / 5)

A small sheltered bay of sand and pebbles beside the old town of Sestri Levante, with pastel houses and calm water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes and restaurants nearby; seasonal sunbed rental. Getting there and parking: Limited parking in town; the bay is a short walk from the centre.

Cala Pira (4.5 / 5)

A sandy cove on the south-east coast of Sardinia near Castiadas, with clear water and Mediterranean scrub behind the beach.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Limited facilities; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking nearby; may be limited in summer.

Torre dei Corsari (4.5 / 5)

A wide sandy bay on the Costa Verde in south-west Sardinia, with dunes and clear water along a long sweep of beach.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Limited facilities; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach; check locally.

Portu Illixi Beach (4.5 / 5)

A quiet sandy bay in south-east Sardinia with clear water, low scrub behind the beach and a wild, unhurried feel.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Limited facilities; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach; check locally.

Mingardo Beach (4.5 / 5)

A quiet cove of small pebbles and clear water on the Cilento coast, set between green cliffs near Marina di Camerota.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Pescara has a wide beach of fine sand running along the city seafront, with long rows of umbrellas and sunbeds and a lively promenade. The water is shallow and warm, and the beach is very popular with Italian families in summer.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Bathing clubs, free sections, showers, bars and lifeguards in season. Getting there and parking: Paid parking along the seafront; the train station is nearby.

About beaches in Italy

Italy's beaches range from the dramatic coves of Sardinia to the colourful, cliff-backed shores of the Amalfi Coast. Sardinia in particular is famous for its clear water and white sand.

When to go

The Italian beach season runs roughly from June to September. Mid-August, around the Ferragosto holiday, is the busiest and most expensive period. June and September give warm water with far fewer people. Outside summer, many beach clubs and some boat services close.

Getting around

Sardinia is reached by ferry or flight and is best explored by car. The Amalfi Coast is served by buses and ferries, and its coastal road is narrow and congested in summer, so public transport or ferries can be easier than driving.

Good to know

Italy uses the euro. Several popular beaches now regulate visitor numbers in summer, including La Pelosa and Cala Goloritze, so check booking rules ahead of time. Many beaches have paid sections with sunbeds alongside free public areas.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to book Italian beaches in advance?

For most beaches, no. But some Sardinian beaches limit daily visitors in summer and require a reservation. See our La Pelosa and Cala Goloritze pages for the details we found.

When is the best time to visit Italian beaches?

June and September are the best balance of warm water and manageable crowds. Avoid mid-August if you want space.
