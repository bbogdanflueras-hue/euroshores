Cala Goloritzé (4.8 / 5)

A secluded cove under a limestone needle, reached only on foot or by boat — worth every step.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: None — carry water and food. Getting there and parking: Su Porteddu car park (included in the ticket), then a steep hike.

Cala Mariolu (4.8 / 5)

A pebble cove with vivid turquoise water below steep cliffs, reachable only by boat or on foot.

Best season: May–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: A small seasonal kiosk — bring water and food. Getting there and parking: No road: boats run from Cala Gonone, Santa Maria Navarrese and Arbatax, or hike about three hours from the Golgo plateau near Baunei.

Spiaggia dei Conigli (4.8 / 5)

A sweep of pale sand and shallow turquoise water on Lampedusa, set inside a nature reserve and often counted among the most beautiful beaches in the Mediterranean.

Best season: June–October. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: None on the beach: no lifeguards, toilets, shade or refreshments, so bring water, food and sun protection. Getting there and parking: About an 800 metre walk from the road, with a bus, bike or scooter to the trailhead. In summer entry is limited to booked shifts through the reserve, so reserve ahead on the official booking site.

Cala Brandinchi (4.8 / 5)

A long bay of fine pale sand and very shallow, clear water backed by stone pines, with Tavolara island rising on the horizon.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Seasonal bar and sunbed hire in summer. Getting there and parking: Paid car parking near the entrance, with a short walk to the beach through the pines. In peak summer, entry and parking can be limited or need advance booking, so check the current rules.

Spiaggia Grande (4.7 / 5)

Nestled beneath the pastel-coloured cliffside village of Positano — one of the most dramatic and romantic settings in Europe.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbed rental, restaurants, showers. Getting there and parking: Limited — use public transport or village car parks.

La Pelosa (4.7 / 5)

A shallow white-sand lagoon facing the Torre della Pelosa at the northwest tip of Sardinia.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbed rental and a snack bar. Getting there and parking: Paid parking in Stintino, then a short walk.

Cala Violina (4.7 / 5)

A crescent of fine pale sand inside the Bandite di Scarlino nature reserve on the Tuscan coast, said to squeak underfoot, which is how it got its name.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: A bar and snack kiosk at the car park, with little at the beach itself and no bins, so carry your litter out. Getting there and parking: Car park at the end of a dirt track off the SS322, then a shaded walk of about 1.5 km through the reserve. From June to September entry is capped and booked online in advance, so check the Scarlino reserve website for current fees.

Cala Luna (4.7 / 5)

A crescent of pale pebbles and coarse sand between limestone cliffs, with caves along the back and a freshwater pond behind the beach.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: Seasonal snack bar in summer; otherwise bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: No road access. Take a boat from Cala Gonone, or walk the coastal trail from Cala Fuili, which is a long hike in both directions.

Tuerredda Beach (4.7 / 5)

A crescent of fine pale sand and shallow turquoise water on the south coast of Sardinia, facing a small island just offshore.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Seasonal kiosk and sunbed rental in summer. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach is usually paid in summer and fills early, so arrive in the morning.

Porto Giunco (4.7 / 5)

A wide sandy beach in the south-east of Sardinia, with an old watchtower on the headland and a lagoon behind. The water is clear and shades from pale to deep turquoise.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Limited facilities; check what is open in season. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach in season; fee unconfirmed.

Baia di Ieranto (4.7 / 5)

A secluded cove on the Sorrentine peninsula, set between steep rocky headlands with deep turquoise water and a rugged, unspoilt shoreline.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: No facilities expected; bring supplies. Getting there and parking: Parking in the village above; the cove is reached on foot.

La Cinta Beach (4.7 / 5)

A long strip of soft white sand and shallow turquoise water just outside San Teodoro, with a lagoon behind it. It is one of the most popular beaches in north-east Sardinia and a favourite for families.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lidos, sunbed hire, paid and free parking. Getting there and parking: Some lots charge a fee and others are free; arrive early in peak season..

Spiaggia di Velluto, Senigallia (4.2 / 5)

Senigallia is known for its Spiaggia di Velluto, the velvet beach, a very wide stretch of fine soft sand with shallow water. The Rotonda a Mare pier stands out in the sea, and the old town is a short walk from the shore.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lidi, showers, cabins, free areas. Getting there and parking: Beach parking not confirmed; check streets near the lungomare..

Baia Verde, Gallipoli (4.2 / 5)

Baia Verde is the beach strip south of the old town of Gallipoli in Puglia, with pale sand, patches of rock and shallow clear water. Bars, beach clubs and holiday apartments sit behind it, and the area is lively in summer with evening music.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lidi, free stretches, paid parking, shuttle. Getting there and parking: Private seasonal lots about 1.50 to 2 EUR per hour; parking is difficult in peak season. Free shuttle from the stadium car park ran 13 June to 31 August in 2026..

Soverato Beach, Calabria (4.2 / 5)

Soverato is a lively seaside town on the Ionian coast of Calabria, with a long beach of pale sand and shallow water. A wide promenade runs behind the sand with bars, shops and restaurants. The beach is popular with Italian families and young people in July and August.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lidi, showers, cabins, bars, free areas nearby. Getting there and parking: Both paid and free parking near the beaches..

Follonica Beach, Tuscany (4.2 / 5)

Follonica is a seaside town in the Maremma on the coast of southern Tuscany, with a long beach of fine sand that slopes gently into the sea. Pine woods and a promenade lie behind the sand, and the town is a classic Italian family resort. The island of Elba is visible across the water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lidi, free beaches, lifeguards. Getting there and parking: Parking areas near lidi, such as at Il Boschetto; public lot fees not confirmed..

Marina Grande Beach (4.1 / 5)

A small public beach of volcanic sand and rocks beside the old fishing harbour of Marina Grande in Sorrento, with colourful houses and restaurants around it.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Seafront restaurants and cafes; the paid sunbed concessions are at nearby Marina Piccola. Getting there and parking: Parking was not confirmed; the walk down from the town is steep and buses or taxis help.

Spiaggia di Sanremo (4.1 / 5)

Sanremo has several public beaches of pebbles and sand along its Riviera coast, with calm water and palm trees on the promenade. The old town of La Pigna and the casino are within walking distance.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lidi, free equipped areas, cabins, showers. Getting there and parking: Seafront parking vouchers sold at some lidi; public parking details not confirmed..

Spiaggia di Scalea (4.1 / 5)

Scalea has a long beach of grey pebbles and sand along the Riviera dei Cedri on the northern Calabrian coast. The sea is clear and warm, and the hills behind the town rise steeply.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lidi, free stretches, paid parking. Getting there and parking: Paid and free areas; use only marked paid bays, beware of unofficial attendants..

Metaponto Beach, Basilicata (4.1 / 5)

Metaponto is a long sandy beach on the Ionian coast of Basilicata, with pine woods behind the dunes and shallow, warm water. The area was a Greek colony in ancient times, and the temple ruins of the Tavole Palatine stand a few kilometres inland.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lidi, free beach, pine woods, showers. Getting there and parking: Parking at lidi such as Il Delfino; public lots not confirmed..

Spiaggia di Rimini (4.0 / 5)

A very wide sandy beach on the Adriatic coast, backed by a long promenade and a large number of bathing establishments. The water is shallow and warm, which makes it popular with families and young groups.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Bathing establishments with sunbeds, bars, restaurants, showers and toilets. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks and street parking in the resort.

Spiaggia di Cattolica (4.0 / 5)

Cattolica is a classic Romagna beach resort, with a very wide strip of fine sand and warm shallow water. It suits families and has a long promenade with plenty of bars and restaurants.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lidi, cabins, bars. Getting there and parking: Parking not confirmed..

Spiaggia di Pescara (4.0 / 5)

Pescara has a wide beach of fine sand running along the city seafront, with long rows of umbrellas and sunbeds and a lively promenade. The water is shallow and warm, and the beach is very popular with Italian families in summer.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lidi, free equipped beach, lifeguards at lidi. Getting there and parking: Parking at some lidi such as Zara; public lots not confirmed..

About beaches in Italy

Italy's beaches range from the dramatic coves of Sardinia to the colourful, cliff-backed shores of the Amalfi Coast. Sardinia in particular is famous for its clear water and white sand.

When to go

The Italian beach season runs roughly from June to September. Mid-August, around the Ferragosto holiday, is the busiest and most expensive period. June and September give warm water with far fewer people. Outside summer, many beach clubs and some boat services close.

Getting around

Sardinia is reached by ferry or flight and is best explored by car. The Amalfi Coast is served by buses and ferries, and its coastal road is narrow and congested in summer, so public transport or ferries can be easier than driving.

Good to know

Italy uses the euro. Several popular beaches now regulate visitor numbers in summer, including La Pelosa and Cala Goloritze, so check booking rules ahead of time. Many beaches have paid sections with sunbeds alongside free public areas.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to book Italian beaches in advance?

For most beaches, no. But some Sardinian beaches limit daily visitors in summer and require a reservation. See our La Pelosa and Cala Goloritze pages for the details we found.

When is the best time to visit Italian beaches?

June and September are the best balance of warm water and manageable crowds. Avoid mid-August if you want space.
