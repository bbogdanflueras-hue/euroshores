Palombaggia (4.8 / 5)

Red granite boulders, parasol pines and soft pale sand — Corsica's most photographed shoreline.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach restaurants, sunbed rental. Getting there and parking: Roadside and paid parking along the coast road.

Saleccia (4.8 / 5)

A long white-sand beach with shallow turquoise water on the wild Agriates coast of Corsica, far from the nearest road.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Minimal — a snack bar in season, otherwise bring food, water and sun protection. Getting there and parking: No road to the sand: shuttle boats run from Saint-Florent, or take a guided 4x4 through the Agriates. Do not drive your own car down the track.

En-Vau Calanque (4.8 / 5)

A small pebble beach at the head of a narrow inlet between white limestone cliffs, with clear turquoise water, in the Calanques National Park near Cassis.

Best season: May–September. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: None on site, so bring water, food and sun protection and take your litter home. Getting there and parking: No road access: reach it on foot by marked trails from the Col de la Gardiole or from Cassis along the GR 51-98, or by boat or kayak.

Plage Notre-Dame (4.8 / 5)

A wide, gently shelving sand beach on car-free Porquerolles, with shallow turquoise water backed by pines and a national park setting.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Very limited at the beach itself, so bring water, food and shade. Getting there and parking: No cars for visitors on the island. Take the short passenger ferry from La Tour Fondue near Hyères, then walk or cycle about 3 km from the village of Porquerolles.

Rondinara Beach (4.7 / 5)

A horseshoe-shaped bay of pale sand and shallow, turquoise water on the south-east coast of Corsica, backed by low scrub and quiet hills.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Seasonal snack bar in summer; otherwise few facilities, so bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Car park behind the beach reached by a signed road from the Bonifacio to Porto-Vecchio route (parking may be charged in summer).

Calanque de Sormiou (4.7 / 5)

A turquoise inlet of pale pebbles and coarse sand wedged between white limestone cliffs in the Calanques National Park, just outside Marseille.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 21 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Small seasonal cafes and restaurants; otherwise bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Car access to Sormiou is restricted in summer, so check the current rules or walk in from the Luminy side, which is a rocky trail of roughly an hour or more. Summer buses may run part of the way, so check the timetable.

Dune du Pilat (4.7 / 5)

A huge sand dune beside the Atlantic at the mouth of Arcachon Bay, with wide beaches and views across the water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 19 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Visitor facilities and food at the foot of the dune. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks near the dune.

La Courtade Beach (4.7 / 5)

A long sandy bay on the car-free island of Porquerolles, with very clear turquoise water and woodland behind the beach.

Best season: May–September. Typical water temperature: about 21 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: No services on the beach; bring water and food. Getting there and parking: No cars on the island; arrival by ferry and then on foot or by bike.

Plage de Roccapina (4.7 / 5)

A wide sandy bay in south-west Corsica, watched over by the Genoese tower and the granite Lion of Roccapina. Access is by a bumpy dirt track, and there are no services, so the beach stays quiet and wild.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: No facilities. Bring food, water and shade. Getting there and parking: A free dirt car park at the end of a rough track of about 2.5 km, with shade in places.

Plage d'Argent (4.7 / 5)

A long, silvery-white beach on Porquerolles, the largest of the Iles d’Hyeres, within a national park and without cars. Pine forest and warm clear water make it feel like the tropics.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: A bar-restaurant, public toilets and a lifeguard post in season. Getting there and parking: No cars on the island; reach by ferry from La Tour Fondue and cycle or walk.

Plage Notre-Dame, Porquerolles (4.7 / 5)

Plage Notre-Dame is a long crescent of white sand on the south-east of Porquerolles, a car-free island off the Var coast, with pine and eucalyptus trees behind and green-turquoise water. The island is a national park, so the sea is clean and clear.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: No shops at the beach; cafes and restaurants in the village by the port. Getting there and parking: No cars on the island; park at Tour Fondue (Giens) or Hyeres and take the ferry.

Santa Giulia (4.6 / 5)

A long white-sand beach on a sheltered bay with shallow, calm water, south of Porto-Vecchio.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lifeguards, toilets, showers, sunbed and water-sport rental, and beachfront cafes. Getting there and parking: Car parks near the beach, which fill early in summer; fees vary.

Calanque de Sugiton (4.6 / 5)

A small rocky cove set between limestone cliffs in the Calanques National Park, reached on foot from Marseille.

Best season: May–September. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: None; bring water and food. Getting there and parking: Parking near Luminy; a hike of about an hour leads down.

Plage de la Palue (4.6 / 5)

A wild sandy beach on the west coast of the Crozon peninsula in Brittany, popular with surfers for its Atlantic waves. A car park on the dune top gives a short walk to the sand.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 17 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: No lifeguards; no services reported on the beach. Getting there and parking: A large car park at the top of the dune, a short walk from the beach.

Plage des Grands Sables (4.6 / 5)

A long sandy beach on the east coast of the island of Groix, famous as one of the few convex beaches in Europe. The shape is created by opposing currents and slowly shifts over time, and the water is clear.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Lifeguards in July and August, a car park by the beach. Getting there and parking: A car park near the beach; most visitors arrive by bike.

Ile de Batz Beach, Roscoff (4.6 / 5)

Ile de Batz is a small car-free island off Roscoff, with white sand coves, clear turquoise water and a garden of exotic plants. The ferry from Roscoff takes about fifteen minutes, and the tide can leave long sand flats at low water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 19 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Bar-restaurant at the Embarcadere beach and a cafe at Porz an Illiz; bring a picnic. Getting there and parking: No car needed on the island: park in Roscoff, take the 15 minute ferry and hire a bike at the port.

Nonza Beach (4.6 / 5)

Nonza beach is a long beach of dark grey pebbles at the foot of the Corsican village of Nonza, on the west side of Cap Corse. The village sits on a cliff above with an old tower, and the sea below is clear and deep close to the shore.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: No facilities on the beach; La Sassa restaurant in the village. Getting there and parking: Very few paid spaces in the village; a rocky staircase of about 15 minutes leads down.

Ostriconi Beach (4.6 / 5)

Ostriconi is a wide sandy beach on the edge of the Desert des Agriates in north-west Corsica, where a small river meets the sea behind the dunes. The water is clear and shallow, and the coast on both sides is wild and undeveloped.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Few confirmed facilities; bring food and water. Getting there and parking: Reached on foot by a path from the former main road; campsite about 1 km away.

Arone Beach, Piana (4.6 / 5)

Arone is a long beach of pale sand at the southern end of the Gulf of Porto in western Corsica, close to the Calanques de Piana. The water is turquoise, the sea floor shelves gently, and wild hills rise behind the beach.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Several restaurants and beach bars; lifeguards in season. Getting there and parking: Car park above the beach; hard to find a space in summer.

Cupabia Beach, Corsica (4.6 / 5)

Cupabia is a long, pale beach in the Gulf of Valinco in south-west Corsica, with a Genoese watchtower close by and maquis-covered hills behind. The water is clear and shallow, and the beach is much quieter than the resorts in the south-east of the island.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: A paillote restaurant and a bar in summer. Getting there and parking: Free car park; steep path to the small cove.

Plage des Dames, Noirmoutier (4.6 / 5)

Plage des Dames is a long beach of fine sand on the island of Noirmoutier, backed by an oak and pine wood and rows of colourful wooden beach huts. The island is linked to the mainland by a bridge and by a tidal causeway called the Passage du Gois. The sea is shallow and the tide goes out far.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Shops and restaurants in town, about 400 m away. Getting there and parking: Paid car park with 17 spaces; free shuttle from town.

Pampelonne Beach (4.5 / 5)

A long arc of fine sand, about 4.5 km, on the Saint-Tropez peninsula, lined with beach clubs, restaurants and water-sports operators.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach clubs with paid sunbeds, restaurants and bars, water-sports hire, and public free stretches. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks along the access roads; traffic to the beach is heavy in July and August.

Calanque de Port-Pin (4.5 / 5)

A narrow limestone inlet between Port-Miou and En-Vau with a small pebble beach and calm turquoise water, reached on foot from Cassis.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 21 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

About beaches in France

Palombaggia, on the southeast coast of Corsica, is among the island's best-known beaches, with fine pale sand, red rocks and umbrella pines behind the shore.

When to go

June to September is the main season. July and August are the busiest, and parking behind the beach can fill early. Late spring and early autumn are quieter, and the sea stays warm into September.

Getting around

Corsica is reached by ferry from mainland France and Italy, or by air to Figari, Bastia, Ajaccio or Calvi. A car is the easiest way to reach the beaches near Porto-Vecchio.

Good to know

France uses the euro. Parts of Corsica's coastline are protected, so follow local signs and take your rubbish away.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to visit Palombaggia?

June or September for warm water and fewer people. July and August are the busiest.

How do I get to Palombaggia?

By car or taxi from Porto-Vecchio. Parking is limited in high season, so arrive early.
