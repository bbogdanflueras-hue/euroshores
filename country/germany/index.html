Beach by beach in Germany

Weststrand, Darss (4.5 / 5)

The Weststrand is a long natural beach inside the Western Pomerania Lagoon Area National Park, backed by forest where storms have bent the trees into strange shapes. There are no buildings, no bars and usually very few people.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: No facilities; this is a protected natural beach. Getting there and parking: Park in Ahrenshoop, Born or Prerow and walk or cycle through the forest (several km).

Binz Beach, Rugen (4.3 / 5)

Binz is the largest resort on Rugen, with a long sweep of fine pale sand in front of a promenade of white villas built in the resort style of around 1900. The water is shallow close to shore, and the pier and the 1968 lifeguard tower by architect Ulrich Muther are local landmarks.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 19 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Hooded wicker beach chairs (Strandkorb) for hire, lifeguards in summer, cafes and restaurants on the promenade. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks in town; trains run to Binz station.

Heringsdorf Beach (4.3 / 5)

Heringsdorf is one of the three Imperial Spas (Kaiserbader) on Usedom, with fine white sand, grand villas and a 508 m pier opened in 1995, the longest in Germany. A promenade runs along the coast to Ahlbeck and Bansin.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 19 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach chairs for hire, lifeguards in summer, shops and restaurants on the pier. Getting there and parking: Paid parking in town; UBB trains stop at Heringsdorf.

Prerow Beach (4.3 / 5)

Prerow has a long, wide beach of fine sand backed by dunes and woods, on the edge of the Western Pomerania Lagoon Area National Park. The village is a quiet holiday spot of thatched houses, and a new pier with a ferry stop opened in 2024.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach chairs for hire, lifeguards in summer, kiosks at the main access points. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks at the edge of the village; buses from Barth and Ribnitz.

Sellin Beach and Pier (4.2 / 5)

Sellin sits on a cliff above the sea, and a long flight of steps or a lift takes you down to the beach and the ornate white pier, rebuilt in 1998 in the style of the original. The sand is fine, the shore shelves gently and beech woods run along the cliffs.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 19 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Beach chairs for hire, lifeguards in summer, restaurant on the pier. Getting there and parking: Paid parking in Sellin; the Rasender Roland steam train stops in the resort.

Warnemunde Beach (4.2 / 5)

Warnemunde has a very wide beach of fine sand next to the old lighthouse and the Teepott building, at the mouth of the river Warnow. Cruise ships and ferries pass close by on their way to Rostock, which makes it a lively place to watch the sea.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach chairs for hire, lifeguards in summer, cafes and fish stalls along the harbour. Getting there and parking: Paid parking; S-Bahn trains run from Rostock to Warnemunde station.

Ahlbeck Beach (4.2 / 5)

Ahlbeck has a broad beach of fine white sand in front of its famous pier, described as the oldest in Germany, with a pavilion with green turrets. The Polish border and Swinoujscie are a short walk along the sand.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 19 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach chairs for hire, lifeguards in summer, restaurant on the pier. Getting there and parking: Paid parking; UBB trains stop at Ahlbeck.

Kuhlungsborn Beach (4.1 / 5)

Kuhlungsborn is a classic Baltic resort with a long sandy beach backed by a seafront promenade, villas and a pier. Groynes break up the shore into sections, and the water is shallow near the beach.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach chairs for hire, lifeguards in summer, restaurants and shops on the promenade. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks; the Molli narrow-gauge steam train links the resort with Bad Doberan.

Timmendorfer Strand (4.1 / 5)

Timmendorfer Strand is one of the best-known resorts on the Bay of Lubeck, with a long beach of fine sand, a promenade and smart hotels. The water is calm and shallow, which suits families.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach chairs for hire, lifeguards in summer, restaurants and shops. Getting there and parking: Paid parking; trains to Timmendorfer Strand station.

Prora Beach (4.0 / 5)

Prora beach runs for kilometres along the Bay of Prorer Wiek, with fine white sand and pine woods behind it. Behind the trees stands the huge unfinished Nazi-era holiday complex, now partly turned into flats, hotels and a youth hostel, with a documentation centre on its history.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 19 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Few facilities on most of the beach; cafes near the converted blocks. Getting there and parking: Car parks along the road between Binz and Mukran.

Travemunde Beach (4.0 / 5)

Travemunde is the seaside quarter of Lubeck, with a sandy beach and promenade at the mouth of the Trave. Large ferries to Scandinavia pass the beach, and the four-masted barque Passat is moored as a museum ship across the river.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach chairs for hire, lifeguards in summer, cafes on the promenade. Getting there and parking: Paid parking; trains from Lubeck to Travemunde Strand station.

About beaches in Germany

Germany has two coasts, but this guide covers the Baltic, where long beaches of fine pale sand run from the Bay of Lubeck past Rostock to the islands of Rugen and Usedom. Grand resort towns with white villas and piers sit beside wild national park beaches backed by forest.

When to go

The season runs from June to early September. July and August are warmest and busiest, with the sea usually around 17 to 20 C. June and September are quieter and often sunny, but the water is cooler.

Getting around

Trains reach most of the big resorts, including Warnemunde, Binz, Timmendorfer Strand and the Usedom resorts on the UBB line. Rostock, Lubeck and Hamburg are the main gateways, and a bike is the best way to explore the Darss and Rugen.

Good to know

Most resorts charge a small visitor tax (Kurtaxe) per night, and some check beach passes in season. Hooded wicker beach chairs (Strandkorb) can be hired by the day. The currency is the euro, and naturist sections (FKK) are common and clearly signed.

Frequently asked questions

Is the German Baltic warm enough to swim?

In July and August the sea is usually around 17 to 20 C, which most people find fresh but fine. Shallow bays warm up first.

What is a Strandkorb?

A hooded wicker beach chair that gives shelter from wind and sun. You can hire one by the day or week on most resort beaches.
