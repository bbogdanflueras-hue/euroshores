Beach by beach in Spain

Cala Macarelleta (4.8 / 5)

A tiny turquoise cove tucked below pine-covered cliffs, a short coastal walk from its bigger neighbour.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: None — bring everything you need. Getting there and parking: Car park at Cala Galdana side, then a coastal trail.

Ses Illetes (4.8 / 5)

A long strip of white sand and shallow, almost Caribbean-coloured water on the northern tip of Formentera.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars and sunbed rental. Getting there and parking: Limited parking; vehicle access can be restricted and charged in season, so many visitors cycle or take the bus.

Praia de Rodas (4.8 / 5)

A pale sand crescent between two islands in the Atlantic Islands of Galicia National Park, with cold, very clear water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 17 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Minimal on the island, so bring your own water, food and shade. Getting there and parking: Reached only by ferry from Vigo, Cangas or Baiona, which take under an hour and run from mid May to mid September in most guides. Daily visitor numbers are capped and a Xunta de Galicia permit is needed, so book the permit and ferry ahead.

Cala Pregonda (4.8 / 5)

A wild north-coast cove of reddish-gold sand framed by rust-coloured rocks and clear green-blue water, with no buildings behind it.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: None, so bring water, food and shade. Getting there and parking: Park at the end of the farm track and walk the sandy coastal path in, which takes roughly 20 to 30 minutes. In summer the car park can be full or restricted, so arrive early and check local notices.

Cala Macarella (4.8 / 5)

A pale sand cove with turquoise water, enclosed by pine-covered cliffs on the south coast of Menorca.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Beach bar and sunbed rental in season. Getting there and parking: Car park above the cove, then a walk down; limited in summer.

Es Trenc (4.7 / 5)

Mallorca's finest untouched beach — miles of pristine white sand, turquoise shallow water, and no high-rise hotels in sight.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars, sunbeds at some access points. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks at the main entrances.

As Catedrais (4.7 / 5)

Towering sea arches and caves carved by the Atlantic along the coast of Galicia, walkable only at low tide.

Best season: May–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Minimal — bring water and sun protection. Getting there and parking: A staircase leads down from the clifftop. A free permit is required from 1 July to 30 September and during Holy Week, booked online at ascatedrais.xunta.es.

Cala Varques (4.7 / 5)

A small sandy cove with clear turquoise water in a pine lined inlet on the east coast of Mallorca, with no bars or other services.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: None: no bars, restaurants or water sports, so bring water, food and shade. Getting there and parking: Parking near the old Ma-4014 road has been restricted with bollards and planned limits, and the rules have changed often. Expect a walk of 1.5 to 2 km from the road on a rough path through woodland.

Playa de Bolonia (4.7 / 5)

A wide, wind-swept Atlantic bay on the Costa de la Luz with a huge sand dune at one end and the Roman ruins of Baelo Claudia just behind the beach.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars (chiringuitos), sunbed rental and a car park, with a private motorhome area on the beach. Getting there and parking: Public car park near the beach. A private paid motorhome area sits right on the sand.

Cala en Turqueta (4.7 / 5)

A small sandy cove with clear green-blue water and pine trees reaching down to the shore on the south-west coast of Menorca.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Few facilities; bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Car park above the cove, then a walk down; limited in summer.

Playa de Sotavento (4.7 / 5)

A very long beach of pale sand on the south of Fuerteventura, with a shallow turquoise lagoon and a sandbar that changes with the tides. Wide, open and often windy.

Best season: April–October. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: A few beach bars and kitesurf schools along the beach; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking areas along the access roads; check locally.

Maspalomas Dunes (4.7 / 5)

A protected field of golden dunes at the southern tip of Gran Canaria, running down to a wide Atlantic beach. Walking through the dunes takes effort but the views are worth it.

Best season: Year-round. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Kiosks and beach bars along the beach edge; check locally. Getting there and parking: Car parks near the lighthouse and beach access; check locally.

Cala de la Granadella (4.6 / 5)

A small cove of pebbles and gravel with very clear water below pine covered cliffs near Xabia, with a clean sandy bottom once you are in the water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Lifeguards and a first aid post in summer, public toilets, hammock and umbrella hire, and a few restaurants behind the beach. Getting there and parking: An authorised car park of around 200 spaces closes to vehicles when full, and in July and August access can be limited, so check the town hall notices. Arriving before 10 am or after 6 pm helps.

Playa de la Concha (4.6 / 5)

A sheltered, shell-shaped city beach with fine golden sand, a long promenade and a small island in the bay, a short walk from the old town of San Sebastian.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbed and umbrella rental in season, showers, changing rooms, lifeguards in summer, plus bars and restaurants close by. Getting there and parking: City beach: walk or use public transport from the centre, as parking in the centre is limited.

Playa de Cofete (4.6 / 5)

A wide, wild beach of golden sand below the Jandia mountains, with strong waves and very few facilities.

Best season: April–October. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: Very few facilities; bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Reached by a long unpaved road; park near the village and check the road condition before you go.

Los Genoveses Beach (4.6 / 5)

A broad, undeveloped sandy bay inside the Cabo de Gata-Nijar natural park, backed by dunes and low hills.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: No built facilities; bring water, shade and food. Getting there and parking: Unpaved parking near the beach, which can be dusty and busy in summer.

Cala Saona (4.6 / 5)

A sheltered sandy cove on the west coast of Formentera with clear shallow water and traditional boat houses on the rocks.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars and sunbeds in season. Getting there and parking: Parking close to the beach, which fills quickly in summer.

Playa de Papagayo (4.6 / 5)

A golden cove at the foot of dark volcanic cliffs on the south coast of Lanzarote. The sheltered water makes it a popular spot for swimming.

Best season: April–October. Typical water temperature: about 21 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Little on the beach itself; bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach; access road and fees unconfirmed.

Calo des Moro (4.6 / 5)

A very small sandy cove squeezed between rocky walls on Mallorca south-east coast, known for its turquoise water.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: No facilities expected; check locally. Getting there and parking: Roadside parking nearby; limited.

Playa de Monsul (4.6 / 5)

A wide golden beach of fine sand backed by dunes and a striking volcanic rock, set inside the Cabo de Gata-Nijar natural park.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: No facilities on the beach; bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Car park near the beach, often full in summer; check locally.

Cala Mitjana (4.6 / 5)

A small, sheltered cove of white sand and clear turquoise water, framed by pine trees and low cliffs on the south coast of Menorca.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: No facilities on the beach; bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Car park a walk from the cove; check locally.

Formentor Beach (4.6 / 5)

A sheltered bay of pale sand and shallow turquoise water at the foot of the Formentor peninsula in northern Mallorca, backed by pine trees.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars, sunbeds and a nearby hotel; check locally. Getting there and parking: Car park near the beach; the Formentor road is busy in summer; check locally.

Cala Mondragó (4.5 / 5)

A sheltered turquoise cove backed by pines inside a natural park, with the sandy bay of S'Amarador alongside.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Showers and a restaurant; walking trails through the natural park. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks at both ends, about a 5–10 minute walk from the sand; they can be full by late morning in high season.

Playa de las Teresitas (4.5 / 5)

A long curved golden beach on the northeast coast of Tenerife, with the Anaga Mountains behind and a breakwater that calms the water.

Best season: Year-round. Typical water temperature: about 21 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Showers, toilets, lifeguards, sunbeds and cafes. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach; busy at weekends and holidays.

Cala Comte (4.5 / 5)

Small sandy coves with very clear water and low islets offshore, on the west coast of Ibiza.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars, sunbeds and umbrellas in season. Getting there and parking: Car park above the beach; fills early in summer.

Cala Mesquida (4.5 / 5)

A wide sandy bay in the north-east of Mallorca, backed by dunes and low hills. The water is clear and the bay is open to the sea, so conditions change with the wind.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Limited facilities that vary by season; sunbed fees unconfirmed. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach in season; fee unconfirmed.

Cala Llombards (4.5 / 5)

A small sandy cove on the south-east coast of Mallorca, set between low rocky cliffs and pine trees with clear shallow water.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Limited facilities; check locally. Getting there and parking: Small car park nearby; fills early in summer.

Cala des Matzoc (4.5 / 5)

A quiet sandy cove on the north-east coast of Mallorca near Arta, with clear water and low cliffs behind the beach.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: No facilities expected; check locally. Getting there and parking: Limited parking nearby.

Cala del Moraig (4.5 / 5)

A pebble cove on the Costa Blanca between steep rocky cliffs, with clear blue water and a rugged, dramatic shoreline.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Facilities nearby; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking near the cove; may be busy in summer.

Cala del Plomo (4.5 / 5)

A remote cove on the Cabo de Gata coast in Andalusia, with pale sand, rocky outcrops and clear water far from the main resorts.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: No facilities expected; bring supplies. Getting there and parking: Reached by a track or path; check access locally.

Playa de Famara (4.5 / 5)

A long, wide sandy beach on the north-west coast of Lanzarote, framed by the dramatic Famara cliffs and popular with surfers and kitesurfers.

Best season: April–October. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes and surf schools nearby; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking near Caleta de Famara; check locally.

Playa de Zahara de los Atunes (4.5 / 5)

A long, wide beach of fine pale sand on Spain's Atlantic coast, with big skies and strong evening light. Wind and surf can be strong on this stretch.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars and restaurants in the village; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking near the village and beach access points; check locally.

Playa de las Canteras (4.5 / 5)

A long, sheltered city beach in Las Palmas, protected by a natural offshore reef that keeps the water calm for most of the year. A promenade runs the whole length.

Best season: Year-round. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes, restaurants and shops along the promenade. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks nearby; check locally.

El Cotillo Beach (4.5 / 5)

A wide sandy beach near the fishing village of El Cotillo on the north-west coast of Fuerteventura, known for clear turquoise water, steady wind and windsurfers.

Best season: April–October. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes and restaurants in El Cotillo village; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking near the village and beach access points; check locally.

Playa de la Barrosa (4.5 / 5)

An 8 km sweep of fine golden sand on the Costa de la Luz, backed by pine woods and a low red cliff at the north end. At low tide the sand flats and rock pools stretch far out, and the sunsets over the Atlantic are the main event.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 21 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Chiringuitos, showers, lifeguards in season, sunbed rental on the central stretch. Getting there and parking: Free and paid car parks along the promenade; fills by midday in summer.

Gulpiyuri Beach (4.4 / 5)

A small sand beach set in a meadow, filled by the sea through underground channels and sheltered by limestone rocks.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: No facilities; services in nearby villages. Getting there and parking: Small parking near the access path.

Playa del Silencio (4.4 / 5)

A quiet cove of dark sand and pebbles tucked below steep green cliffs on the Asturian coast.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: None; bring water and food. Getting there and parking: Small car park near the village; a short walk and steps lead down.

Cala Salada (4.4 / 5)

A sheltered cove on the west coast of Ibiza, with clear green-blue water and pine-covered slopes.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Small beach restaurants in season; sunbeds and umbrellas. Getting there and parking: Car park above the cove; busy in summer.

Platja de Muro (4.4 / 5)

A long beach of fine sand in the Bay of Alcudia, backed by dunes and an old watchtower. The water usually stays calm in the bay.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, umbrellas and beach bars along parts of the beach (fees unconfirmed). Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach entrances; fee unconfirmed.

Barceloneta Beach (4.4 / 5)

The long sandy city beach of Barcelona, next to Port Vell and the Barceloneta district.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Showers and bars nearby; details unconfirmed. Getting there and parking: Limited; public transport recommended.

Cala Bassa (4.4 / 5)

A wide sandy bay on the west coast of Ibiza with calm, shallow water backed by pine trees.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach clubs, sunbeds and food nearby; fees unconfirmed. Getting there and parking: Car park nearby; may be busy in summer.

Platja de Son Real (4.4 / 5)

A natural sandy beach on the north-east coast of Mallorca, backed by dunes, scrub and a small coastal lagoon, with clear shallow water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Limited facilities; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking some distance from the beach; check locally.

La Fosca Beach (4.4 / 5)

A sheltered sandy beach on the Costa Brava near Palamos, with calm clear water and rocky coves at either end.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes and services nearby; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking nearby; may be busy in summer.

Playa de Cabopino (4.4 / 5)

A sandy beach on the Costa del Sol beside the small Cabopino marina and an old watchtower, backed by pine woods and dunes. Part of the beach is used by naturists.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars and restaurants near the marina; check locally. Getting there and parking: Car park near the marina; check locally.

Playa de Zarautz (4.4 / 5)

The longest beach on the Basque coast, about 2.5 km of golden sand backed by a promenade of bars and restaurants. Several surf schools are based here and the waves suit learners as well as experienced surfers.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lifeguard, sunshade and lounger rental, toilets, showers and surf schools. Getting there and parking: Parking in town, within about ten minutes on foot of the sand.

Cala Tarida (4.4 / 5)

A west-facing bay on Ibiza with clear water and a curve of sand, popular for evening sunsets. Beach bars and restaurants sit on and above the beach.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars and restaurants, sunbed and parasol hire. Getting there and parking: Small car park above the beach; the access road backs up by early afternoon in summer.

Aiguablava Beach (4.4 / 5)

A small sheltered cove near Begur on the Costa Brava, with pine-covered cliffs and clear water. The Parador de Aiguablava stands on the cliff above the beach.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Toilets, adapted showers, water sports and a summer beach restaurant. Getting there and parking: Car park beside the Parador, paid in summer.

Ses Salines (4.4 / 5)

A long sandy beach at the southern tip of Ibiza, inside the Ses Salines natural park with dunes and pine woods behind. Beach clubs and bars line parts of the sand.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Loungers and parasols, beach clubs, bars and restaurants. Getting there and parking: Small free lot by the road that fills early; larger paid car park near the roundabout.

Son Moll Beach (4.2 / 5)

A broad sandy beach beside the town of Cala Ratjada in north-east Mallorca, with rows of sunbeds and hotels behind. The water is clear and the town is a short walk away.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas, with cafes and shops in town (fees unconfirmed). Getting there and parking: Public parking in Cala Ratjada; fee unconfirmed.

Estanyol Beach (4.2 / 5)

A small sandy beach on the north-east coast of Mallorca with rocky edges, calm clear water and a relaxed local feel.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Facilities nearby; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking nearby; check locally.

Playa de Calahonda (4.2 / 5)

A small cove in the old town of Nerja, reached through a little arch behind the Balcon de Europa and a zigzag of steps down to the sand. Cliffs frame the beach and the cafes of the town centre are a short walk above.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Sunbeds, showers, toilets and a lifeguard in high season. Getting there and parking: No parking at the beach; use the town car parks and walk to the Balcon de Europa.

About beaches in Spain

Our Spanish picks are all in the Balearic Islands, where pine-fringed coves, white sand and shallow turquoise water make for some of the most photogenic beaches in the western Mediterranean.

When to go

June to September is the main season, with the water warmest in August and September. July and August are the busiest, and the most accessible beaches fill early. May and October can be pleasant but cooler, and some beach bars and services only open from late spring.

Getting around

Each island has an airport, and renting a car is the easiest way to reach the quieter coves of Mallorca and Menorca. Formentera is reached by ferry, and cycling or renting a scooter is a popular way to get around there.

Good to know

Spain uses the euro. Parking near popular coves is limited, and some areas restrict vehicle access in summer, so arrive early. Posidonia seagrass meadows protect the water here, so avoid anchoring on them if you are on a boat.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to visit the Balearic beaches?

Late June and September are warm with fewer crowds than July and August.

Do I need a car in the Balearic Islands?

On Mallorca and Menorca a car is the easiest way to reach quiet coves. On Formentera, bikes and scooters are popular.
