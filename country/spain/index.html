Cala Macarelleta (4.8 / 5)

A tiny turquoise cove tucked below pine-covered cliffs, a short coastal walk from its bigger neighbour.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: None — bring everything you need. Getting there and parking: Car park at Cala Galdana side, then a coastal trail.

Ses Illetes (4.8 / 5)

A long strip of white sand and shallow, almost Caribbean-coloured water on the northern tip of Formentera.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars and sunbed rental. Getting there and parking: Limited parking; vehicle access can be restricted and charged in season, so many visitors cycle or take the bus.

Praia de Rodas (4.8 / 5)

A pale sand crescent between two islands in the Atlantic Islands of Galicia National Park, with cold, very clear water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 17 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Minimal on the island, so bring your own water, food and shade. Getting there and parking: Reached only by ferry from Vigo, Cangas or Baiona, which take under an hour and run from mid May to mid September in most guides. Daily visitor numbers are capped and a Xunta de Galicia permit is needed, so book the permit and ferry ahead.

Cala Pregonda (4.8 / 5)

A wild north-coast cove of reddish-gold sand framed by rust-coloured rocks and clear green-blue water, with no buildings behind it.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: No bar, toilets or sunbeds on the sand; bring water and food. A restaurant near the Binimel-la car park. Getting there and parking: Park at Binimel-la, about 2 km by path; the lot fills early and cars spill along the road.

Cala Macarella (4.8 / 5)

A pale sand cove with turquoise water, enclosed by pine-covered cliffs on the south coast of Menorca.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Colonia de Sant Jordi is a quiet seaside village on the south coast of Mallorca with a small beach of fine sand and clear shallow water, and a harbour with fishing boats. Longer beaches such as Es Trenc and Es Carbo lie a short drive away, and boats leave for the island of Cabrera.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Son Bou Beach, Menorca (4.3 / 5)

Platja del Regueral, Cambrils (4.2 / 5)

Platja del Morro de Gos is a calm bay of sand and fine gravel in Oropesa del Mar, with clear shallow water. The Morro de Gos headland shelters it, and the old town and castle sit on the hill above.

Our Spanish picks are all in the Balearic Islands, where pine-fringed coves, white sand and shallow turquoise water make for some of the most photogenic beaches in the western Mediterranean.

When to go

June to September is the main season, with the water warmest in August and September. July and August are the busiest, and the most accessible beaches fill early. May and October can be pleasant but cooler, and some beach bars and services only open from late spring.

Getting around

Each island has an airport, and renting a car is the easiest way to reach the quieter coves of Mallorca and Menorca. Formentera is reached by ferry, and cycling or renting a scooter is a popular way to get around there.

Good to know

Spain uses the euro. Parking near popular coves is limited, and some areas restrict vehicle access in summer, so arrive early. Posidonia seagrass meadows protect the water here, so avoid anchoring on them if you are on a boat.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to visit the Balearic beaches?

Late June and September are warm with fewer crowds than July and August.

Do I need a car in the Balearic Islands?

On Mallorca and Menorca a car is the easiest way to reach quiet coves. On Formentera, bikes and scooters are popular.
