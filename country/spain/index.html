Beach by beach in Spain

Cala Macarelleta (4.8 / 5)

A tiny turquoise cove tucked below pine-covered cliffs, a short coastal walk from its bigger neighbour.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: None — bring everything you need. Getting there and parking: Car park at Cala Galdana side, then a coastal trail.

Ses Illetes (4.8 / 5)

A long strip of white sand and shallow, almost Caribbean-coloured water on the northern tip of Formentera.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars and sunbed rental. Getting there and parking: Limited parking; vehicle access can be restricted and charged in season, so many visitors cycle or take the bus.

Praia de Rodas (4.8 / 5)

A pale sand crescent between two islands in the Atlantic Islands of Galicia National Park, with cold, very clear water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 17 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Minimal on the island, so bring your own water, food and shade. Getting there and parking: Reached only by ferry from Vigo, Cangas or Baiona, which take under an hour and run from mid May to mid September in most guides. Daily visitor numbers are capped and a Xunta de Galicia permit is needed, so book the permit and ferry ahead.

Cala Pregonda (4.8 / 5)

A wild north-coast cove of reddish-gold sand framed by rust-coloured rocks and clear green-blue water, with no buildings behind it.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: None, so bring water, food and shade. Getting there and parking: Park at the end of the farm track and walk the sandy coastal path in, which takes roughly 20 to 30 minutes. In summer the car park can be full or restricted, so arrive early and check local notices.

Cala Macarella (4.8 / 5)

A pale sand cove with turquoise water, enclosed by pine-covered cliffs on the south coast of Menorca.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Beach bar and sunbed rental in season. Getting there and parking: Car park above the cove, then a walk down; limited in summer.

Es Trenc (4.7 / 5)

Mallorca's finest untouched beach — miles of pristine white sand, turquoise shallow water, and no high-rise hotels in sight.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars, sunbeds at some access points. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks at the main entrances.

As Catedrais (4.7 / 5)

Towering sea arches and caves carved by the Atlantic along the coast of Galicia, walkable only at low tide.

Best season: May–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Minimal — bring water and sun protection. Getting there and parking: A staircase leads down from the clifftop. A free permit is required from 1 July to 30 September and during Holy Week, booked online at ascatedrais.xunta.es.

Cala Varques (4.7 / 5)

A small sandy cove with clear turquoise water in a pine lined inlet on the east coast of Mallorca, with no bars or other services.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: None: no bars, restaurants or water sports, so bring water, food and shade. Getting there and parking: Parking near the old Ma-4014 road has been restricted with bollards and planned limits, and the rules have changed often. Expect a walk of 1.5 to 2 km from the road on a rough path through woodland.

Playa de Bolonia (4.7 / 5)

A wide, wind-swept Atlantic bay on the Costa de la Luz with a huge sand dune at one end and the Roman ruins of Baelo Claudia just behind the beach.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars (chiringuitos), sunbed rental and a car park, with a private motorhome area on the beach. Getting there and parking: Public car park near the beach. A private paid motorhome area sits right on the sand.

Cala en Turqueta (4.7 / 5)

A small sandy cove with clear green-blue water and pine trees reaching down to the shore on the south-west coast of Menorca.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Few facilities; bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Car park above the cove, then a walk down; limited in summer.

Cala de la Granadella (4.6 / 5)

A small cove of pebbles and gravel with very clear water below pine covered cliffs near Xabia, with a clean sandy bottom once you are in the water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Lifeguards and a first aid post in summer, public toilets, hammock and umbrella hire, and a few restaurants behind the beach. Getting there and parking: An authorised car park of around 200 spaces closes to vehicles when full, and in July and August access can be limited, so check the town hall notices. Arriving before 10 am or after 6 pm helps.

Playa de la Concha (4.6 / 5)

A sheltered, shell-shaped city beach with fine golden sand, a long promenade and a small island in the bay, a short walk from the old town of San Sebastian.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbed and umbrella rental in season, showers, changing rooms, lifeguards in summer, plus bars and restaurants close by. Getting there and parking: City beach: walk or use public transport from the centre, as parking in the centre is limited.

Playa de Cofete (4.6 / 5)

A wide, wild beach of golden sand below the Jandia mountains, with strong waves and very few facilities.

Best season: April–October. Typical water temperature: about 20 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: Very few facilities; bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Reached by a long unpaved road; park near the village and check the road condition before you go.

Los Genoveses Beach (4.6 / 5)

A broad, undeveloped sandy bay inside the Cabo de Gata-Nijar natural park, backed by dunes and low hills.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 22 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: No built facilities; bring water, shade and food. Getting there and parking: Unpaved parking near the beach, which can be dusty and busy in summer.

Cala Saona (4.6 / 5)

A sheltered sandy cove on the west coast of Formentera with clear shallow water and traditional boat houses on the rocks.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars and sunbeds in season. Getting there and parking: Parking close to the beach, which fills quickly in summer.

Playa de Papagayo (4.6 / 5)

A golden cove at the foot of dark volcanic cliffs on the south coast of Lanzarote. The sheltered water makes it a popular spot for swimming.

Best season: April–October. Typical water temperature: about 21 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Little on the beach itself; bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach; access road and fees unconfirmed.

Calo des Moro (4.6 / 5)

A very small sandy cove squeezed between rocky walls on Mallorca south-east coast, known for its turquoise water.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: No facilities expected; check locally. Getting there and parking: Roadside parking nearby; limited.

Cala Mondragó (4.5 / 5)

A sheltered turquoise cove backed by pines inside a natural park, with the sandy bay of S'Amarador alongside.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Showers and a restaurant; walking trails through the natural park. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks at both ends, about a 5–10 minute walk from the sand; they can be full by late morning in high season.

Playa de las Teresitas (4.5 / 5)

A long curved golden beach on the northeast coast of Tenerife, with the Anaga Mountains behind and a breakwater that calms the water.

Best season: Year-round. Typical water temperature: about 21 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Showers, toilets, lifeguards, sunbeds and cafes. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach; busy at weekends and holidays.

Cala Comte (4.5 / 5)

Small sandy coves with very clear water and low islets offshore, on the west coast of Ibiza.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars, sunbeds and umbrellas in season. Getting there and parking: Car park above the beach; fills early in summer.

Cala Mesquida (4.5 / 5)

A wide sandy bay in the north-east of Mallorca, backed by dunes and low hills. The water is clear and the bay is open to the sea, so conditions change with the wind.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Limited facilities that vary by season; sunbed fees unconfirmed. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach in season; fee unconfirmed.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

A quiet sandy cove on the north-east coast of Mallorca near Arta, with clear water and low cliffs behind the beach.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

A remote cove on the Cabo de Gata coast in Andalusia, with pale sand, rocky outcrops and clear water far from the main resorts.

About beaches in Spain

Our Spanish picks are all in the Balearic Islands, where pine-fringed coves, white sand and shallow turquoise water make for some of the most photogenic beaches in the western Mediterranean.

When to go

June to September is the main season, with the water warmest in August and September. July and August are the busiest, and the most accessible beaches fill early. May and October can be pleasant but cooler, and some beach bars and services only open from late spring.

Getting around

Each island has an airport, and renting a car is the easiest way to reach the quieter coves of Mallorca and Menorca. Formentera is reached by ferry, and cycling or renting a scooter is a popular way to get around there.

Good to know

Spain uses the euro. Parking near popular coves is limited, and some areas restrict vehicle access in summer, so arrive early. Posidonia seagrass meadows protect the water here, so avoid anchoring on them if you are on a boat.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to visit the Balearic beaches?

Late June and September are warm with fewer crowds than July and August.

Do I need a car in the Balearic Islands?

On Mallorca and Menorca a car is the easiest way to reach quiet coves. On Formentera, bikes and scooters are popular.
