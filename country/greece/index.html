Beach by beach in Greece

Navagio Beach (4.9 / 5)

The iconic Shipwreck Beach, enclosed by soaring white limestone cliffs — arguably the most photographed beach in the world.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 26 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: None on the beach — bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Normally boat only (from Porto Vromi or Zakynthos town), but landings are currently banned — view it from the cliff platform instead.

Myrtos Beach (4.9 / 5)

Consistently ranked among Europe's most beautiful beaches — gleaming white pebbles meet the most intense shade of turquoise blue imaginable, framed by towering cliffs.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 27 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Small cafe, sunbeds, viewpoint car park. Getting there and parking: Viewpoint car park above; winding road down.

Elafonissi Beach (4.8 / 5)

A rare pink-sand lagoon at the southwestern tip of Crete, where shallow crystal-clear waters meet rose-tinted sands.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, tavernas, toilets. Getting there and parking: Large car park near the beach.

Balos Lagoon (4.8 / 5)

A shallow lagoon of pale pink sand and impossible blues at the tip of the Gramvousa peninsula.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Canteen, sunbed rental in season. Getting there and parking: Unpaved road to the car park, then a stepped path.

Porto Katsiki (4.8 / 5)

Tall white cliffs plunge into bright turquoise water on the Ionian coast of Lefkada, reached by a long flight of steps from the clifftop.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Canteen and restaurant above the beach; little shade, so bring an umbrella. Getting there and parking: Paid clifftop car park (fills early), then a stairway down to the beach.

Voidokilia Beach (4.8 / 5)

A near perfect omega shaped bay of fine sand on the Messinian coast, with calm shallow water and the Gialova lagoon wildlife area just behind it.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: None on the sand, so bring your own shade, water and food. Getting there and parking: By car to the parking area at the end of the track, then a short walk of around 100 metres; the area can fill up on summer weekends.

Egremni Beach (4.8 / 5)

A long sweep of pale sand and vivid turquoise water below white limestone cliffs on the west coast of Lefkada.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: None at the beach, so bring water, food and shade. Getting there and parking: By car to the top of the cliff, then a steep staircase of roughly 350 steps (sources give between 300 and 420) rebuilt after the 2015 earthquake. Boat trips from Vasiliki and Nidri are the other way in, so check current conditions before you go.

Sarakiniko (4.7 / 5)

A lunar shoreline of smooth white volcanic rock sculpted by wind and sea, with deep clear water for swimming from the rocks.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Very limited — bring water, a hat and sun protection. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach (fills early in summer); local buses also run from Adamas.

Simos Beach (4.7 / 5)

Two wide bays of fine pale sand and shallow turquoise water, separated by a strip of dunes on the small island of Elafonisos.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, beach bars and tavernas in summer. Getting there and parking: Take the short ferry from Pounta on the Peloponnese to Elafonisos, then drive or take a local bus to the beach, where parking is available nearby but fills in peak season.

Falassarna Beach (4.7 / 5)

A long, wide beach of pale sand and clear shallow water on the far west coast of Crete, known for its sunsets.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas in parts; tavernas nearby. Getting there and parking: Roadside and informal parking near the beach.

Lalaria Beach (4.7 / 5)

A striking beach of smooth white pebbles beneath tall cliffs on the north coast of Skiathos, with sea arches and caves nearby. It is usually visited on a boat trip.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: No facilities expected; check locally. Getting there and parking: No road access; usually reached by boat.

Porto Timoni (4.7 / 5)

Two back-to-back coves on the north-west coast of Corfu, separated by a narrow strip of land. The only way in is on foot from the village of Afionas along a rocky path.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: No facilities reported on the beach; tavernas in Afionas. Getting there and parking: Limited parking in Afionas fills fast in summer; one guide mentions a paid lot of about 5 EUR at the trailhead.

Kolona Beach (4.7 / 5)

A narrow strip of fine sand joining the main island of Kythnos to the islet of Agios Loukas, with water on both sides. It is widely described as the most famous beach on the island.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Limited: a beach bar or taverna on part of the beach, reported with some sunbeds; little natural shade. Getting there and parking: Parking is at Fikiada, not at the beach itself; a rough dirt road runs from Chora.

Falasarna Beach (4.7 / 5)

A broad golden beach on the far west coast of Crete, with clear turquoise water and views toward the Gramvousa peninsula. It is known for its sunsets and has room for everyone even in summer.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, umbrellas, tavernas, toilets and showers. Getting there and parking: Free parking near the beach and along the access road.

Preveli Beach (4.6 / 5)

A cove of sand and pebbles where a river meets the sea, below steep cliffs on the south coast of Crete.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: Few facilities; bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Car park above the beach, then a steep path and steps down.

Seitan Limani (4.6 / 5)

A tiny sandy inlet squeezed between steep rock walls on the Akrotiri peninsula, with deep turquoise water.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: No facilities; bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Parking above the cove, then a steep, rocky path down.

Koukounaries Beach (4.6 / 5)

A wide sandy bay fringed by pine trees on the south-west coast of Skiathos, with a small lagoon behind the beach.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, umbrellas, tavernas and water sports in season. Getting there and parking: Car park near the beach; fills early in summer.

Glyka Nera Beach (4.6 / 5)

A pebble cove on the south coast of Crete beneath steep cliffs, known for cool freshwater springs that mix with the clear sea.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: Limited facilities; check locally. Getting there and parking: No road access; reached on foot or by boat.

Kleftiko (4.6 / 5)

A cluster of white rock arches, caves and sheltered coves on the south-west coast of Milos, once used as a pirate hideout. There is no road and no beach facilities, so you swim and snorkel from a boat.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: None on land; boat tours provide water, snacks and snorkel gear. Getting there and parking: Not applicable; reached only by boat from Adamas or Agia Kiriaki.

Kavourotrypes Beach (4.6 / 5)

A string of small coves on the east coast of Sithonia, also known as Orange Beach. White rocks frame clear turquoise water, with small patches of sand between the outcrops.

Best season: May–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: A few sunbeds and a small beach bar near the main cove; a toilet near the car park; no showers or changing cabins. Getting there and parking: Free parking under the trees above the beach; it fills quickly in July and August.

Apella Beach (4.6 / 5)

A pretty bay of white pebbles and very clear water on the east coast of Karpathos, set under green hills. It is about 20 km from Pigadia, and the last part of the trip is on a winding road that ends above the cove.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: A small taverna, sunbed and umbrella sets for rent, no shops. Getting there and parking: A small car park by the taverna where the road ends, then a short path down to the beach.

Katergo Beach (4.6 / 5)

A wild, unspoilt cove on the south-east side of Folegandros, backed by tall cliffs and with deep blue water. There are no facilities, which is a large part of its charm.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: None. Bring your own water, food and shade. Getting there and parking: No parking at the beach; park at Livadi or Karavostasi and walk or take the boat.

Mylos Beach (4.6 / 5)

A wild, mostly pebbly beach south of Agios Nikitas, framed by steep green hills and bright blue water. A forest path or a short boat ride keeps it feeling remote.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: One bar with sunbed and umbrella rental, little else. Getting there and parking: No beach parking; park in Agios Nikitas and walk about 1 km.

Tsambika Beach (4.5 / 5)

A long golden-sand crescent on the east coast of Rhodes, backed by a rocky headland and a hilltop monastery, with warm shallow water and a lively line of beach tavernas.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, umbrellas, showers, changing rooms, tavernas, beach bars and water sports. Getting there and parking: Drive to the free car park close to the sand. The monastery above has its own car park and about 300 to 350 steps up.

Vai Beach (4.5 / 5)

A sandy bay backed by a large natural palm grove on the far east coast of Crete.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, tavernas and basic facilities in season. Getting there and parking: Car parks near the beach.

Plaka Beach (4.5 / 5)

A very long sand beach on the southwest coast of Naxos, backed by dunes and open to the Aegean.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Some sunbeds and tavernas near the access roads. Getting there and parking: Free parking near the main access points.

Agios Prokopios Beach (4.5 / 5)

A long sandy beach on the west coast of Naxos, with shallow turquoise water and views across to Paros.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, umbrellas, tavernas and water sports in season. Getting there and parking: Parking along the road behind the beach; town buses run in summer.

Kokkinokastro Beach (4.5 / 5)

A pebble beach on the east coast of Alonnisos, named for its red cliffs, with rocky islets just offshore.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Limited; a taverna or two nearby in season. Getting there and parking: Roadside parking; the road can be rough.

Mikro Alyko Beach (4.5 / 5)

A small sandy cove on the south-west coast of Naxos, with clear turquoise water and a rocky headland on one side.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Limited facilities; bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach; fee unconfirmed.

Gerakas Beach (4.5 / 5)

A sandy bay on the south-east coast of Zakynthos below pale clay cliffs. It lies within a protected area used by nesting sea turtles, so local rules apply.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Limited facilities; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach; check local rules.

Red Beach Santorini (4.5 / 5)

A pebble beach on the south coast of Santorini near Akrotiri, set below tall red volcanic cliffs with deep blue water.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas likely available; fees unconfirmed. Getting there and parking: Parking near the access path; limited in summer.

Golden Beach Paros (4.5 / 5)

A long sandy beach on the east coast of Paros, open to the wind and popular with windsurfers and kitesurfers in summer.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Facilities in season; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking nearby; check locally.

Santa Maria Beach (4.5 / 5)

A sandy bay on the north coast of Paros near Naoussa, with clear water and a relaxed summer atmosphere.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Facilities in season; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking nearby; check locally.

Matala Beach (4.5 / 5)

A curved sandy bay on the south coast of Crete, overlooked by a sandstone cliff riddled with ancient caves that give the beach its unmistakable look.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas in season; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking is available near the village; check locally.

Petani Beach (4.5 / 5)

A long beach of pale sand and pebbles beneath white limestone cliffs on the west coast of the Paliki peninsula, with clear water and a relaxed atmosphere.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: A few tavernas and some sunbeds in season; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking above the beach with a short walk down; check locally.

Paleokastritsa Beach (4.5 / 5)

A cluster of small coves on the north-west coast of Corfu, backed by green hills and cliffs. Agios Spyridon is the main beach, and boats from the harbour run to sea caves and quieter coves.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas, tavernas, showers, toilets and boat rental. Getting there and parking: Paid car park behind the main beach; it fills early in summer.

Kolymbithres Beach (4.5 / 5)

A beach near Naoussa on Paros, known for its smooth, sculpted rock formations that separate small sandy coves. The water is shallow and calm, which suits families.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas, beach clubs, tavernas nearby, water sports. Getting there and parking: Free parking nearby, but spaces are limited; a bus or water taxi runs from Naoussa.

Mylopotas Beach (4.5 / 5)

A wide horseshoe bay of golden sand, about 2 km from Chora on Ios. It is the island's busiest beach, with beach bars and tavernas along the shore and hills behind.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas, beach bars, tavernas, water sports and shops nearby. Getting there and parking: No dedicated public parking was found; a bus runs from Chora every 15 to 20 minutes in peak season, or you can walk.

Agios Sostis Beach (4.5 / 5)

A quiet sandy beach on the north coast of Mykonos, far from the busy clubs of the south. The beach is undeveloped, with no organised sunbeds and a single taverna nearby.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: No sunbeds or umbrellas to rent; a taverna nearby. Getting there and parking: Roadside parking on the narrow access road; arrive early.

Kalamitsi Beach (4.5 / 5)

A string of pebble coves on the west coast of Lefkada below the village of Kalamitsi, with clear water under steep cliffs. Kavalikefta, Megali Petra and Avali lie close together along the same rough road.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas for rent at Kavalikefta with a restaurant above the beach; a snack bar at Avali; nothing at Megali Petra. Getting there and parking: A small private car park at the end of the road at Kavalikefta; limited parking at Avali.

Mikri Vigla Beach (4.5 / 5)

Long, pale beaches on the south-west coast of Naxos, split into two bays by a small rocky headland. The steady summer wind makes Mikri Vigla one of the island hubs for kitesurfing and windsurfing, and the water is clear and turquoise.

Best season: May–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

About beaches in Greece

Greece has more coastline than almost any other European country, spread across the mainland and thousands of islands. The beaches range from pink-tinted lagoons in Crete to dramatic cliff-backed coves in the Ionian islands, and the sea is clear and calm for most of the summer.

When to go

July and August are the hottest and busiest months, and the most popular beaches fill up by late morning. May, June and September are warmer than most people expect and much quieter, and the sea is usually at its warmest from late summer into early autumn. The meltemi, a strong northerly wind, blows across the Aegean in July and August, so beaches on the Ionian side can be calmer on windy days.

Getting around

Most islands are reached by ferry or a short flight from Athens or Thessaloniki. Renting a car or scooter is the easiest way to reach remote beaches on islands such as Crete and Kefalonia, although some roads are steep, narrow or unpaved. Boat trips run to beaches such as Balos on Crete, and a few places can only be reached by sea.

Good to know

The currency is the euro. Many organised beaches rent sunbeds and umbrellas, but plenty have no facilities at all, so carry water and shade. Access rules at protected or hazardous sites can change from season to season, so check locally before you travel.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to visit Greek beaches?

For warm water and fewer crowds, aim for late May to June or September. July and August are hot and busy, and the meltemi wind can make exposed Aegean beaches rough.

Which Greek island has the best beaches?

There is no single answer. Crete has the pink sand of Elafonissi and Balos, Zakynthos has the Navagio cliffs (check current access rules), and Kefalonia has Myrtos.

Do I need a car to reach Greek beaches?

On larger islands a car or scooter helps a lot. Some beaches are reached by boat only, and some are best visited early to avoid the day-boat crowds.
