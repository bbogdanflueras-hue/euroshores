Beach by beach in Poland

Leba Beach (4.3 / 5)

Leba is a busy resort town with a broad beach of very fine white sand. Just west of town is the Slowinski National Park, a UNESCO biosphere reserve famous for moving sand dunes, the highest about 40 m.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 17 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lifeguarded sections in summer, beach bars, sunbed and screen hire. Getting there and parking: Paid parking in town; trains to Leba in summer.

Sopot Beach (4.2 / 5)

Sopot has a wide sandy beach between Gdansk and Gdynia, with the 511 m wooden pier, the longest in Europe, and the grand 1920s Grand Hotel right on the sand. The resort is lively in summer, with bars and clubs along the seafront.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lifeguarded sections in summer, beach bars, sunbed and screen hire, toilets. Getting there and parking: Paid parking is tight; SKM trains run from Gdansk and Gdynia to Sopot.

Hel Beach (4.2 / 5)

The Hel Peninsula is a narrow 35 km spit of sand between the open Baltic and the Bay of Puck. On the sea side, the beach near the town of Hel is wide and backed by pine forest, and the town has a seal centre run by the University of Gdansk.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lifeguarded sections in summer, kiosks, toilets near the main entrances. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks in Hel; trains and summer ferries from Gdynia and Gdansk.

Debki Beach (4.2 / 5)

Debki is a small village with a wide, natural beach of fine sand and dunes, where the Piasnica river flows into the Baltic. It is popular with young people and families who want a simpler holiday than in the big resorts.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 17 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lifeguarded section in summer, beach bars at the main entrance. Getting there and parking: Paid car parks in the village.

Miedzyzdroje Beach (4.2 / 5)

Miedzyzdroje is a popular resort on the island of Wolin, with a wide sandy beach, a pier and a seafront promenade. To the east, tall cliffs and beech forest belong to Wolin National Park, home to a small bison reserve.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lifeguarded sections in summer, beach bars, toilets, sunbed and screen hire. Getting there and parking: Paid parking; trains from Szczecin and Swinoujscie.

Swinoujscie Beach (4.2 / 5)

Swinoujscie has a very wide beach of fine white sand on the Polish side of Usedom, with a long promenade that continues into Germany towards Ahlbeck. The town sits on several islands, linked by ferries and a road tunnel.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lifeguarded sections in summer, beach bars, toilets, sunbed and screen hire. Getting there and parking: Paid parking near the promenade.

Orlowo Beach and Cliff (4.1 / 5)

Orlowo is a quieter beach in Gdynia, below the Orlowo Cliff, a wooded clifftop nature reserve with views across the Bay of Gdansk. A small pier and fishing huts sit at the southern end.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Lifeguarded section in summer, a few cafes near the pier. Getting there and parking: Limited street parking; SKM trains to Gdynia Orlowo station.

Jurata Beach (4.1 / 5)

Jurata is the smartest village on the Hel Peninsula. You can choose between the open-sea beach with waves on the north side and the calm, shallow Bay of Puck on the south side, where a pier runs out into the water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lifeguarded sections in summer, cafes and restaurants in the village. Getting there and parking: Paid parking; trains along the peninsula stop at Jurata.

Kolobrzeg Beach (4.1 / 5)

Kolobrzeg is one of the largest spa towns on the Polish coast, with a long sandy beach, a pier and a red-brick lighthouse at the harbour mouth. Spa hotels and parks line the seafront.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 18 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lifeguarded sections in summer, beach bars, toilets, sunbed and screen hire. Getting there and parking: Paid parking; trains from Szczecin and Koszalin.

Ustka Beach (4.0 / 5)

Ustka is an old fishing port with sandy beaches on both sides of the harbour, a lighthouse and a long promenade. The eastern beach is the main resort area, and the western beach is quieter.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 17 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Lifeguarded sections in summer, cafes, sunbed and screen hire. Getting there and parking: Paid parking; trains from Slupsk.

About beaches in Poland

Poland has about 440 km of Baltic coast, almost all of it fine white sand backed by dunes and pine forest. Highlights range from the lively Tricity resorts of Sopot and Gdynia to the Hel Peninsula, the moving dunes near Leba and the cliffs of Wolin.

When to go

The season runs from late June to the end of August, when lifeguards are on duty and the sea is usually around 17 to 19 C. Weekends in July and August are busy, and early September is calmer.

Getting around

Gdansk, Szczecin and Bydgoszcz have the nearest airports. Trains reach Sopot, Gdynia, Hel, Leba in summer, Ustka, Kolobrzeg, Miedzyzdroje and Swinoujscie, and summer ferries cross from Gdynia and Gdansk to Hel.

Good to know

The currency is the Polish zloty, and cards are widely accepted. Windbreak screens (parawany) are a Polish beach tradition and can often be hired. Swim in guarded areas marked by flags, as currents can be strong on the open sea.

Frequently asked questions

When is the Polish Baltic warmest?

Late July and August, with the sea usually around 18 C. Shallow bays such as the Bay of Puck can be warmer.

Which Polish beaches are best for families?

Calm, shallow water on the Bay of Puck side of the Hel Peninsula suits small children, and the big resorts have guarded beaches and facilities.
