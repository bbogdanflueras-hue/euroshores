Beach by beach in Montenegro

Sveti Stefan (4.5 / 5)

A fairytale island-village connected to the mainland by a narrow isthmus of pink pebbles and crystal-clear Adriatic water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, cafes nearby. Getting there and parking: Roadside and paid parking above the beach.

Valdanos Bay (4.5 / 5)

A crescent-shaped bay of rounded pebbles, roughly 300 metres long, tucked below old olive groves on the south coast of Montenegro near Ulcinj.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Very limited, with a small seasonal snack bar at best, so bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: By car or boat from Ulcinj; the last stretch is on a rough track, so check road conditions and expect to walk the final part.

Drobni Pijesak (4.5 / 5)

A small cove of pale pebbles and coarse sand, about 250 metres long, set between olive trees and low rocky headlands on the Budva Riviera south of Sveti Stefan.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas for hire, a beach bar and nearby restaurants in season, and a freshwater spring beside the beach. Getting there and parking: By car on a winding road with free parking close by; the last stretch is a steep set of steps, so it suits less well anyone with limited mobility or a stroller.

Queens Beach (4.5 / 5)

Two small coves of pink-tinged pebbles and sand below a wooded park on the Budva Riviera, with calm clear water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas in season; access may be managed by the adjoining hotel. Getting there and parking: Parking near the Milocer park; limited in summer.

Ada Bojana Beach, Montenegro (4.5 / 5)

Ada Bojana is a triangular island formed by the mouth of the Bojana river at the border with Albania, with a long beach of dark golden sand on the sea side. Wooden beach restaurants and a naturist area stand on the island, and fishermen use old stilt huts on the river. The sea is shallow and warm.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 26 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars, restaurants and rentals. Getting there and parking: Reports differ: EUR 5 per car, or EUR 2 per hour up to EUR 10 per day.

Velika Plaža, Ulcinj (4.5 / 5)

Velika Plaža (Big Beach) is a sandy strip of roughly 12 km (sources give 12 to 14 km) that runs from Ulcinj south to the Bojana river near Ada Bojana. The water stays shallow for a long way out, and steady afternoon winds make it one of the leading kitesurfing spots in the Balkans, with several schools at the southern end. Dunes and a protected natural area sit behind the sand, with beach bars and restaurants at intervals.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars, restaurants, showers, toilets and changing rooms in places; kitesurf schools at the south end. Getting there and parking: Parking near the main access points; can be tight in peak season.

Becici Beach (4.4 / 5)

A long, curving beach on the Budva Riviera with mountains rising behind the resort town.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, beach bars and water sports in season. Getting there and parking: Paid and street parking in Becici; limited in summer.

Mogren Beach (4.4 / 5)

Mogren is a pair of small coves just beside the old town of Budva, joined by a footpath cut into the cliffs. The beaches are made of fine pebbles and coarse sand, and the water is clear.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars, sunbeds and umbrellas. Getting there and parking: Limited informal parking uphill near the road to Mogren Fortress; walking from the old town is advised.

Zanjice Beach, Lustica (4.4 / 5)

Zanjice is a pebble bay on the Lustica peninsula at the mouth of the Bay of Kotor, with a view of Mamula island. The water is clear and boat trips run to the nearby Blue Cave. A restaurant and beach bar sit behind the shore, and the road down is narrow.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bar, restaurant, toilets and sunbeds. Getting there and parking: Small car park at the bay, limited in summer.

Jaz Beach (4.3 / 5)

One of the longest beaches in Montenegro, a wide pebble sweep about 2.5 km west of Budva with room to spread out.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: A campsite behind the beach; other services vary by season. Getting there and parking: Road access about 2.5 km west of central Budva; check parking on arrival in peak season.

Kamenovo (4.3 / 5)

A long pebble-and-sand beach on the Budva Riviera, framed by green hills and calm, clear water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, cafes and water sports in summer. Getting there and parking: Parking along the coastal road is limited in peak season, so arrive early or come by local bus or taxi from Budva.

Buljarica Beach (4.3 / 5)

A long pebble beach near Petrovac with green hills behind it and a quieter feel than the busier Budva beaches.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Seasonal beach bars and sunbeds; otherwise basic. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach and along the road; fees vary, so check locally.

Ulcinj Mala Plaza (4.3 / 5)

A small town beach below the old town of Ulcinj, with a sandy shore and views of the rocky headland.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars and sunbed rental nearby in season. Getting there and parking: Street and public parking in Ulcinj; limited in summer.

Sutomore Beach (4.3 / 5)

A pebble beach town and rocky coast between Bar and Petrovac, with clear water below steep hills.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars and sunbed rental in the main beach area. Getting there and parking: Parking in Sutomore town; limited in summer.

Petrovac Beach (4.3 / 5)

A long curve of fine pebbles and sand in front of the town of Petrovac, with two small islets offshore and hills behind.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, umbrellas, cafes and restaurants along the promenade. Getting there and parking: Town car parks and street parking; busy in July and August.

Rafailovici Beach, Budva Riviera (4.3 / 5)

Rafailovici is a seaside village on the Budva Riviera with a long beach of small pebbles and a promenade that leads to the neighbouring village of Becici. The water is clear and deep blue, and green hills rise behind the shore. Cafes and restaurants line the beach.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, bars and restaurants. Getting there and parking: Shaded lot at the entrance about EUR 5 per day (review); paid in peak season.

Plavi Horizonti Beach, Lustica (4.3 / 5)

Plavi Horizonti is a wide, clean beach on the Lustica peninsula, with fine pebbles, some sand and very clear water. The sea gets deep gradually. Beach bars and restaurants are set behind the shore and there is parking nearby.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Bar, restaurant, showers, toilets and sunbeds. Getting there and parking: Parking available near the beach.

Przno Beach (4.2 / 5)

A small sheltered bay on the Budva Riviera with sunbeds along a shore of pebbles and sand, and the village of Przno behind.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, umbrellas and restaurants in season. Getting there and parking: Small car parks in the village; limited in peak season.

Zukovica Beach (4.2 / 5)

A small cove on the Montenegrin coast with a narrow shore of pebbles and rocks, wooded slopes behind and clear water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Limited facilities; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking nearby; check locally.

Čanj Beach, Bar (4.2 / 5)

Čanj is a small resort on the coast of Bar Municipality in southern Montenegro. The beach is about 1 km long and mostly sand mixed with some pebbles, and its main stretch is known as Biserna Obala (the Pearl Coast). Hotels, cottages, bars and restaurants line the shore and are busiest in summer, and there are showers and toilets nearby.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Bars, restaurants, showers and toilets. Getting there and parking: Free car park nearby; fills up in peak season.

Veliki Pijesak Beach, Ulcinj (4.1 / 5)

Veliki Pijesak is a sandy beach near Ulcinj on the south Montenegrin coast, with shallow warm water that suits families. Beach bars and cafes line the shore in summer. The wider Ulcinj area is known for its long sandy beaches and warm sea.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars, showers, toilets and sunbeds in season. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach, usually paid in season.

About beaches in Montenegro

Sveti Stefan is a small fortified islet joined to the mainland by a narrow causeway, with pink-tinted sand on the beaches beside it. It sits on the Budva Riviera on Montenegro's Adriatic coast.

When to go

June to September is the main season. July and August are warm and busy, and September is warm but calmer. The islet itself is a resort area, so check what access is available before you plan your visit.

Getting around

Budva is the nearest large town, and Tivat and Podgorica have airports. Buses run along the coast, and a car or taxi is the easiest way to reach the beaches.

Good to know

Montenegro uses the euro, although it is not an EU member. Some beach areas are run by hotels and charge for sunbeds, so check on arrival.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to visit Sveti Stefan?

June or September for warm weather and fewer people than July and August.

Can you visit the island of Sveti Stefan?

Access to the islet has been limited because it is a resort. Check current access rules locally.
