Beach by beach in Croatia

Zlatni Rat (4.8 / 5)

The famous "Golden Horn" — a distinctive pebble cape that juts into the Adriatic and magically shifts shape with the currents and winds.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 26 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Water sports, beach bars, showers. Getting there and parking: Car park in Bol, short walk.

Stiniva (4.7 / 5)

A hidden pebble cove squeezed between sheer cliffs, with a narrow sea entrance framing the turquoise water.

Best season: May–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: Minimal — bring water, shade and snacks. Getting there and parking: Trailhead above the cove, then a steep path of about 20 minutes down; boat tours also visit.

Sakarun Beach (4.6 / 5)

A long, shallow, white-sand bay in the Kornati archipelago — rare for the Adriatic and ideal for families.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bar, sunbed rental. Getting there and parking: Parking above the bay.

Punta Rata (4.6 / 5)

A fine-pebble beach backed by pine forest, with Brela Rock rising from the sea at its western end.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Showers, restaurants, a beach bar, sunbed and umbrella hire, changing rooms and summer lifeguards. Getting there and parking: A short walk from Brela village; check parking in peak season.

Dubovica (4.6 / 5)

A small pebble cove on the south coast of Hvar, reached by a steep path from the road or by boat.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: A few small seasonal beach bars; limited shade, so bring cash and an umbrella. Getting there and parking: Roadside parking only, just off the Stari Grad–Hvar road, then a steep walk of 10–15 minutes down; the strip can be full by about 09:00 in July and August.

Saplunara (4.6 / 5)

A rare sandy bay at the southeast end of Mljet, relatively quiet and set in protected surroundings.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Minimal — a cafe bar with deck chairs in season and a mobile toilet. Getting there and parking: Reached by taxi or bus from Sobra; there are no hotels or large developments.

Rajska Plaza (4.6 / 5)

Also called Paradise Beach, the best-known beach on Rab, a long stretch of brown sand where the warm, shallow water reaches far out.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbed and umbrella hire, showers, changing cabins, bars and restaurants, lifeguards and water sports. Getting there and parking: Reached from Lopar village; check parking in peak season.

Pasjaca Beach (4.6 / 5)

A wild pebble beach below a steep cliff in Konavle, south-east of Dubrovnik, known for its dramatic setting.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: Few or no facilities; bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Park near the top and walk down a steep path and steps to the beach.

Nugal Beach (4.6 / 5)

A small, secluded pebble cove with clear turquoise water below limestone cliffs and pines, reached on foot along a path from the Makarska coast.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: No facilities; bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Parking in town and a walk along the coastal path; check locally.

Sunj Beach (4.5 / 5)

A shallow crescent of sand about 200 metres long, backed by pines on the car free island of Lopud, a rare sandy beach near Dubrovnik.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Sunbed and umbrella hire, showers, toilets and a couple of beach bars in season, with kayak and paddleboard hire. Getting there and parking: There are no cars on the island. Take the Elaphiti ferry from Gruz harbour in Dubrovnik (about 45 minutes), then walk 25 to 30 minutes through the forest or take a golf cart taxi in season.

Slanica Beach (4.5 / 5)

A sandy bay near the town of Murter with shallow water and pine trees along the shore.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes and beach services nearby in season. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach in season.

Porat Beach (4.5 / 5)

A small sandy bay on the island of Bisevo, with very clear water, a few houses and terraced fields behind.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: A small konoba in season; otherwise bring supplies. Getting there and parking: Reached by boat or on foot from the village; no car park confirmed.

Banje Beach (4.4 / 5)

A pebble beach just outside the Old Town walls, with a paid beach club and free public sections on both sides.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Showers, changing rooms, toilets, sunbed hire, nearby bars and water-sports rental. Getting there and parking: A short walk east of the Old Town; there is little parking nearby.

Stoncica Beach (4.4 / 5)

A sheltered pebble bay on the island of Vis, with clear water and pine-covered hills around it.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Seasonal bar or restaurant nearby; otherwise basic. Getting there and parking: Roadside parking near the bay; you need a ferry to reach Vis.

Pinizule Beach (4.4 / 5)

A small rocky cove on the Istrian coast with clear water, flat stone slabs and pine trees reaching down to the shore.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: No facilities at the cove; seasonal cafes nearby. Getting there and parking: Parking near the Kamenjak area; entry fee may apply.

Zrce Beach (4.4 / 5)

A long pebble beach on the island of Pag, about 2 km from Novalja, known for its beach clubs and summer music events. Mornings are calmer; evenings are lively.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach clubs and bars in season; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach; check locally.

Lone Bay (4.4 / 5)

A pebble and rock bay with clear water, shaded by pines in the Zlatni Rt forest park just south of Rovinj, with paths along the coast.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes, sunbeds and a promenade nearby; check locally. Getting there and parking: Car parks near the park, often busy in summer; check locally.

Ovcice Beach (4.3 / 5)

A pebble and sand beach beside the Marjan peninsula in Split, with pine trees behind and clear water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes and a beach bar in season; showers nearby. Getting there and parking: City parking nearby is limited; walking or the bus is easier.

Supetar Beach (4.3 / 5)

A pebble beach near the town of Supetar on the island of Brac, with clear water and views across the channel.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes and shops nearby in town; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking in town; may be busy in summer.

Bačvice (4.2 / 5)

Split's city beach, with shallow, sandy-bottomed water and the home of picigin, a ball game played in the shallows.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Showers and changing rooms at both ends, sunbed and umbrella hire, and cafes and bars along the promenade. Getting there and parking: In central Split, a short walk from the old town; city parking is limited.

Suluci Beach (4.0 / 5)

A small rocky cove right beside the old town of Dubrovnik, with deep clear water and views of the walls.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: None; bring water and a towel. Getting there and parking: City parking is limited; walk from the old town.

About beaches in Croatia

Croatia's Adriatic coast is known for clear water, pebble beaches and hundreds of islands. Zlatni Rat on Brac and Sakarun are two of its best-known beaches.

When to go

June to September is the best time, with sea temperatures peaking in July and August. Late June and September are quieter and still warm. Ferries run more often in summer, which makes island hopping easier to plan.

Getting around

Ferries and catamarans link the islands to Split and other coastal ports, and car ferries run on the busier routes. Many beaches are a short walk or drive from the nearest town.

Good to know

Croatia has used the euro since 2023. Many Croatian beaches are pebble or rock, so water shoes are useful. Strong winds such as the bora and jugo can affect sailing and ferry services.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to visit Croatian beaches?

June to September, with late June and September being warm and less crowded.

Are Croatian beaches sandy?

Mostly not. Pebble and rock are far more common than sand, and the water is usually very clear. Sakarun is better known for fine pebbles and shallow water.
