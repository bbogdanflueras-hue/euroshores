Beach by beach in Croatia

Zlatni Rat (4.8 / 5)

The famous "Golden Horn" — a distinctive pebble cape that juts into the Adriatic and magically shifts shape with the currents and winds.

Best season: May–October. Typical water temperature: about 26 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Water sports, beach bars, showers. Getting there and parking: Car park in Bol, short walk.

Stiniva (4.7 / 5)

A hidden pebble cove squeezed between sheer cliffs, with a narrow sea entrance framing the turquoise water.

Best season: May–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: Minimal — bring water, shade and snacks. Getting there and parking: Trailhead above the cove, then a steep path of about 20 minutes down; boat tours also visit.

Punta Rata Beach (4.7 / 5)

A long arc of small white pebbles under pine trees in Brela, with clear turquoise water and a rock off the shore. It is regularly named among the best beaches in Croatia.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds and umbrellas, beach bars, free showers in the village. Getting there and parking: Paid beach car park; cheaper options at nearby hotels and the village.

Sakarun Beach (4.6 / 5)

A long, shallow, white-sand bay in the Kornati archipelago — rare for the Adriatic and ideal for families.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bar, sunbed rental. Getting there and parking: Parking above the bay.

Punta Rata (4.6 / 5)

A fine-pebble beach backed by pine forest, with Brela Rock rising from the sea at its western end.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Showers, restaurants, a beach bar, sunbed and umbrella hire, changing rooms and summer lifeguards. Getting there and parking: A short walk from Brela village; check parking in peak season.

Dubovica (4.6 / 5)

A small pebble cove on the south coast of Hvar, reached by a steep path from the road or by boat.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: A few small seasonal beach bars; limited shade, so bring cash and an umbrella. Getting there and parking: Roadside parking only, just off the Stari Grad–Hvar road, then a steep walk of 10–15 minutes down; the strip can be full by about 09:00 in July and August.

Saplunara (4.6 / 5)

A rare sandy bay at the southeast end of Mljet, relatively quiet and set in protected surroundings.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Minimal — a cafe bar with deck chairs in season and a mobile toilet. Getting there and parking: Reached by taxi or bus from Sobra; there are no hotels or large developments.

Rajska Plaza (4.6 / 5)

Also called Paradise Beach, the best-known beach on Rab, a long stretch of brown sand where the warm, shallow water reaches far out.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbed and umbrella hire, showers, changing cabins, bars and restaurants, lifeguards and water sports. Getting there and parking: Reached from Lopar village; check parking in peak season.

Pasjaca Beach (4.6 / 5)

A wild pebble beach below a steep cliff in Konavle, south-east of Dubrovnik, known for its dramatic setting.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: Few or no facilities; bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Park near the top and walk down a steep path and steps to the beach.

Nugal Beach (4.6 / 5)

A small, secluded pebble cove with clear turquoise water below limestone cliffs and pines, reached on foot along a path from the Makarska coast.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Challenging.

Facilities: No facilities; bring water and shade. Getting there and parking: Parking in town and a walk along the coastal path; check locally.

Pupnatska Luka Beach, Korcula (4.6 / 5)

Pupnatska Luka is a long pebble bay on the south coast of Korcula, below the village of Pupnat, with clear turquoise water and forested hills on both sides. It is one of the best-known beaches and stays quiet outside the peak weeks.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: A restaurant above the bay in season; limited facilities on the beach. Getting there and parking: Car park above the bay with a short walk down; the access road is steep and narrow.

Sunj Beach (4.5 / 5)

A shallow crescent of sand about 200 metres long, backed by pines on the car free island of Lopud, a rare sandy beach near Dubrovnik.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Sunbed and umbrella hire, showers, toilets and a couple of beach bars in season, with kayak and paddleboard hire. Getting there and parking: There are no cars on the island. Take the Elaphiti ferry from Gruz harbour in Dubrovnik (about 45 minutes), then walk 25 to 30 minutes through the forest or take a golf cart taxi in season.

Slanica Beach (4.5 / 5)

A sandy bay near the town of Murter with shallow water and pine trees along the shore.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes and beach services nearby in season. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach in season.

Porat Beach (4.5 / 5)

A small sandy bay on the island of Bisevo, with very clear water, a few houses and terraced fields behind.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: A small konoba in season; otherwise bring supplies. Getting there and parking: Reached by boat or on foot from the village; no car park confirmed.

Nin Beach (4.5 / 5)

Queen Beach (Kraljicina plaza) is a sandy beach of more than 2 km on the Nin lagoon near Zadar. The water stays shallow for up to about 500 m from the shore, which suits small children. The lagoon bed is sand and mud and shifts with the weather.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sun loungers and umbrellas for rent, and a beach bar nearby. Getting there and parking: Public parking in the small pine forest; paid in summer, reported free in September.

Sveti Jakov Beach (4.5 / 5)

A small pebble and shingle cove east of Dubrovnik old town, with clear, deep blue water and wooded cliffs behind. It is calmer than the beaches nearest the walls, and the view of the coast is lovely.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: A beach bar and restaurant, sunbed and umbrella rental, toilets and showers. Getting there and parking: Limited parking near the church above the beach; city buses 5 and 8 run along the main road.

Kamenjak Beach, Premantura (4.5 / 5)

Kamenjak is a protected peninsula at the southern tip of Istria, with rocky coves, small pebble beaches and low pine scrub. The sea is clear and deep, and the beaches are quiet and wild compared with the busy resorts nearby.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Few facilities; small seasonal bars at some coves. Getting there and parking: Paid park entry for cars and bikes in season; walk or cycle the dirt tracks.

Lokrum Island, Dubrovnik (4.5 / 5)

Lokrum is a small wooded island a short boat ride from the old port of Dubrovnik, with rocky shores, a botanical garden and a ruined monastery. A small salt-water lake called Mrtvo More, or the Dead Sea, has calm water for swimming, and the open sea is very clear.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: A cafe and restaurant, toilets, walking paths and a botanical garden; swim from rock platforms. Getting there and parking: No cars on the island; boats leave from the old port at regular times in season.

Palmizana Beach, Pakleni Islands (4.5 / 5)

Palmizana is a bay on Sveti Klement, the largest of the Pakleni Islands off Hvar, with a pine-shaded shore of rock slabs and small pebble coves, a marina and a botanical garden. The water is clear and calm, and the bay is a popular stop for sailors.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars, restaurants, a marina and rock platforms; sunbeds at some points. Getting there and parking: No cars on the island; water taxis run from Hvar town in season.

Soline Beach, Brela (4.5 / 5)

Soline is a pebble beach beside the village of Brela on the Makarska Riviera, shaded by pines, with clear turquoise water and the Biokovo mountains behind. It sits next to the better-known Punta Rata beach and is a little quieter.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, parasols, cafes and water sports in season. Getting there and parking: Paid parking in Brela and along the main road; limited in July and August.

Banje Beach (4.4 / 5)

A pebble beach just outside the Old Town walls, with a paid beach club and free public sections on both sides.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Showers, changing rooms, toilets, sunbed hire, nearby bars and water-sports rental. Getting there and parking: A short walk east of the Old Town; there is little parking nearby.

Stoncica Beach (4.4 / 5)

A sheltered pebble bay on the island of Vis, with clear water and pine-covered hills around it.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Seasonal bar or restaurant nearby; otherwise basic. Getting there and parking: Roadside parking near the bay; you need a ferry to reach Vis.

Pinizule Beach (4.4 / 5)

A small rocky cove on the Istrian coast with clear water, flat stone slabs and pine trees reaching down to the shore.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: No facilities at the cove; seasonal cafes nearby. Getting there and parking: Parking near the Kamenjak area; entry fee may apply.

Zrce Beach (4.4 / 5)

A long pebble beach on the island of Pag, about 2 km from Novalja, known for its beach clubs and summer music events. Mornings are calmer; evenings are lively.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach clubs and bars in season; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking near the beach; check locally.

Lone Bay (4.4 / 5)

A pebble and rock bay with clear water, shaded by pines in the Zlatni Rt forest park just south of Rovinj, with paths along the coast.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes, sunbeds and a promenade nearby; check locally. Getting there and parking: Car parks near the park, often busy in summer; check locally.

Stara Baska Beach (4.4 / 5)

A long bay at the southern tip of Krk with fine gravel and pebbles and clear water, framed by bare limestone hills. One road runs through the village.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: One guide lists showers, a beach bar, restaurants, sun loungers, umbrellas and changing rooms. Getting there and parking: Limited roadside parking; several beaches sit before the village entrance and are reached on foot.

Zavala Beach (4.4 / 5)

A pebble and gravel beach in the village of Zavala on the quiet south coast of Hvar. The village is reached from Pitve through a tunnel, with a signposted car park near the beach.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Konoba Zavala, Zaca Bar and Restaurant and Restaurant Davor nearby; I found no sunbed rental details. Getting there and parking: A signposted car park near the beach.

Kasjuni Beach (4.4 / 5)

A pebble beach below the forested Marjan hill on the south side of Split, with clear water and a lively beach lounge. It sits about 3 km from the old town and is a favourite local escape.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bar and lounge, umbrella and chair rental, toilets and showers. Getting there and parking: A managed car park that fills quickly in summer; city bus 12 also runs here.

Makarska Beach (4.4 / 5)

The long pebble and sand town beach of Makarska, with the dramatic Biokovo mountain wall behind it and a Mediterranean promenade along the back. Pine shade, water sports and cafes make it easy to spend a whole day.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, water sports, lockers, restaurants and cafes. Getting there and parking: City paid parking; hotels may charge separately.

Jagodna Beach (4.4 / 5)

A pebble beach in a clear bay on the south coast of Hvar, east of Hvar Town, backed by pine forest. It is quieter than the town beaches, and the water is clear and sheltered.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 25 degrees C. Access: Moderate.

Facilities: Limited facilities; a small bar and sunbeds may operate in season. Getting there and parking: Roadside parking; limited spaces.

Trstenica Beach (4.4 / 5)

Trstenica is a long beach of fine shingle and sand beside the town of Orebic on the Peljesac peninsula. The water is shallow and clear, with views across the channel to the island of Korcula.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 23 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, beach bars, showers, water sports and lifeguards in season. Getting there and parking: Free and paid roadside parking near the beach.

Zlatni Rt, Rovinj (4.4 / 5)

Zlatni Rt, or Punta Corrente, is a forest park of pines and cypresses south of Rovinj old town, with pebble and rock beaches along its shore. The water is clear and deep close to the edge, and paths run through the trees to small coves.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars, sunbeds in places, showers and a cycle and walking path. Getting there and parking: Paid parking at the park entrance; the old town is a short walk.

Cikat Bay, Losinj (4.4 / 5)

Cikat is a long, sheltered bay beside Mali Losinj, with pine and tamarisk trees casting shade along a shore of rock platforms and small pebble beaches. The water is clear and calm, and a seafront path links the bay to the town.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Beach bars, sunbeds, water sports, showers and a promenade in season. Getting there and parking: Paid parking near the bay; the town centre is a short walk.

Sjekirica Beach, Brsecine (4.4 / 5)

Sjekirica is a small pebble beach on the Adriatic coast north of Dubrovnik, close to the village of Brsecine, with a rocky islet just offshore and green hills behind. The water is very clear, and the beach is quiet because it is away from the main roads.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: No sunbeds; a small kiosk in season; limited facilities. Getting there and parking: Small free parking area at the top; a short walk down a path.

Tucepi Beach (4.4 / 5)

Tucepi is a long pebble beach on the Makarska Riviera in central Dalmatia, below the Biokovo mountains. The water is clear, pines and palms line the seafront, and there are bars, restaurants and water sports along the shore.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, parasols, bars, restaurants, showers and water sports in season. Getting there and parking: Paid parking in town and along the seafront; limited in July and August.

Donje Celo Beach, Kolocep (4.4 / 5)

Donje Celo is a small village on Kolocep, the smallest inhabited Elaphiti island near Dubrovnik, with a small sandy beach in a sheltered bay. There are no cars on the island, and boats from Dubrovnik make the crossing a short ride.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: A cafe and a few restaurants in the village; limited facilities on the beach. Getting there and parking: No cars on the island; take a boat from Dubrovnik and walk from the landing.

Gradac Beach, Makarska Riviera (4.4 / 5)

Gradac is a pebble beach town at the southern end of the Makarska Riviera, with a long promenade, clear water and the Biokovo mountains rising steeply behind. It is quieter than the resorts further north and has a relaxed, family feel.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Sunbeds, parasols, cafes, restaurants and water sports in season. Getting there and parking: Paid parking in town and along the seafront; limited in July and August.

Ovcice Beach (4.3 / 5)

A pebble and sand beach beside the Marjan peninsula in Split, with pine trees behind and clear water.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes and a beach bar in season; showers nearby. Getting there and parking: City parking nearby is limited; walking or the bus is easier.

Supetar Beach (4.3 / 5)

A pebble beach near the town of Supetar on the island of Brac, with clear water and views across the channel.

Best season: June–September. Typical water temperature: about 24 degrees C. Access: Easy.

Facilities: Cafes and shops nearby in town; check locally. Getting there and parking: Parking in town; may be busy in summer.

Pakostane Beach (4.3 / 5)

About beaches in Croatia

Croatia's Adriatic coast is known for clear water, pebble beaches and hundreds of islands. Zlatni Rat on Brac and Sakarun are two of its best-known beaches.

When to go

June to September is the best time, with sea temperatures peaking in July and August. Late June and September are quieter and still warm. Ferries run more often in summer, which makes island hopping easier to plan.

Getting around

Ferries and catamarans link the islands to Split and other coastal ports, and car ferries run on the busier routes. Many beaches are a short walk or drive from the nearest town.

Good to know

Croatia has used the euro since 2023. Many Croatian beaches are pebble or rock, so water shoes are useful. Strong winds such as the bora and jugo can affect sailing and ferry services.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to visit Croatian beaches?

June to September, with late June and September being warm and less crowded.

Are Croatian beaches sandy?

Mostly not. Pebble and rock are far more common than sand, and the water is usually very clear. Sakarun is better known for fine pebbles and shallow water.
