Planning your visit to Suluci Beach

Rocky coves often have clear, deeper water that suits snorkelling, but the entry can be slippery. Wear water shoes, enter slowly and avoid swimming when there is a swell.

Access is rated moderate: expect stairs, a short walk or a rough road, and wear proper shoes rather than flip-flops.

The usual season runs June–September. Visiting near the start or end of that window usually means fewer people, but the sea is cooler and some facilities may not be open yet or may have closed for the year.

Best months to visit Suluci Beach

The swimming season here runs from June to September. July, August are the warmest and busiest, with the sea around 23 °C, so arrive early for parking and a good spot. June and September are quieter and cooler: good for walking and photos, though some beach bars and lifeguard posts may not be open yet or may already have closed for the year.

Who Suluci Beach suits

A rocky shore suits confident swimmers and snorkellers more than families with small children; bring water shoes. Expect a short walk or uneven ground, so it suits most visitors with sensible footwear. There are few or no facilities on the sand, so bring water, food and shade.

Getting there and nearby

City parking is limited; walk from the old town. The beach sits at about 42.641, 18.104 on the map, so use the map link above for directions. If you are planning a day around it, the closest other beaches on our list are Banje Beach (1 km), Kupari Beach (7 km), Sunj Beach (13 km).
