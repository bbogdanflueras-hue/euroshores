Planning your visit to Cala Mariolu

Pebble beaches usually mean clear water but awkward walking. Bring water shoes and a thick towel or mat to sit on.

Access is rated challenging and involves a steep hike. Take plenty of water, start early and allow enough daylight for the walk back.

The usual season runs May–September. Visiting near the start or end of that window usually means fewer people, but the sea is cooler and some facilities may not be open yet or may have closed for the year. We flag this beach as a hidden gem, so it is quieter than the headline beaches nearby, but it is no secret: arrive early in peak summer.

Best months to visit Cala Mariolu

The swimming season here runs from May to September. June, July, August are the warmest and busiest, with the sea around 23 °C, so arrive early for parking and a good spot. May and September are quieter and cooler: good for walking and photos, though some beach bars and lifeguard posts may not be open yet or may already have closed for the year.

Who Cala Mariolu suits

Pebbles and clear water suit swimmers and snorkellers; bring water shoes for an easier entry. Access is demanding, so it suits fit, well-prepared visitors; wear sturdy shoes and carry water. It is flagged as a hidden gem, so it suits travellers who prefer quieter places over the headline beaches. Food and drink close by make it easy to stay for the whole day.

Getting there and nearby

No road: boats run from Cala Gonone, Santa Maria Navarrese and Arbatax, or hike about three hours from the Golgo plateau near Baunei. The beach sits at about 40.123, 9.676 on the map, so use the map link above for directions. If you are planning a day around it, the closest other beaches on our list are Cala Goloritzé (2 km), Cala Sisine (7 km), Cala Luna (12 km).
