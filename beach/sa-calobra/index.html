Planning your visit to Sa Calobra

Pebble beaches usually mean clear water but awkward walking. Bring water shoes and a thick towel or mat to sit on.

Access is rated moderate: expect stairs, a short walk or a rough road, and wear proper shoes rather than flip-flops.

The usual season runs May–October. Visiting near the start or end of that window usually means fewer people, but the sea is cooler and some facilities may not be open yet or may have closed for the year.

Best months to visit Sa Calobra

The swimming season here runs from May to October. June, July, August, September are the warmest and busiest, with the sea around 23 °C, so arrive early for parking and a good spot. May and October are quieter and cooler: good for walking and photos, though some beach bars and lifeguard posts may not be open yet or may already have closed for the year.

Who Sa Calobra suits

Pebbles and clear water suit swimmers and snorkellers; bring water shoes for an easier entry. Expect a short walk or uneven ground, so it suits most visitors with sensible footwear. Food and drink close by make it easy to stay for the whole day.

Getting there and nearby

A paid car park at the port fills quickly in summer; boats also run from Port de Soller. The beach sits at about 39.851, 2.800 on the map, so use the map link above for directions. If you are planning a day around it, the closest other beaches on our list are Formentor Beach (30 km), Platja de Muro (30 km), Platja de Son Real (37 km).
