Playa de Carnota

4.5 / 5 One of the longest beaches in Galicia below Monte Pindo in Carnota, A Coruna, Galicia · Hidden gem

Playa de Carnota is one of the longest beaches in Galicia, a wide stretch of fine pale sand on the Costa da Morte, with the granite slopes of Monte Pindo rising behind. A lagoon and wetland sit behind the dunes, and the beach stays uncrowded outside August.

Planning your visit to Playa de Carnota

Sand beaches are usually gentle underfoot and easy for swimmers and children, though they can be exposed to wind and waves, so check conditions on the day. Bring a hat and extra water, as shade can be scarce away from facilities.

Access is rated easy, so most visitors can reach the beach without a long walk or special equipment.

The usual season runs June–September. Visiting near the start or end of that window usually means fewer people, but the sea is cooler and some facilities may not be open yet or may have closed for the year. We flag this beach as a hidden gem, so it is quieter than the headline beaches nearby, but it is no secret: arrive early in peak summer.

Best months to visit Playa de Carnota

The swimming season here runs from June to September. July, August are the warmest and busiest, with the sea around 20 °C, so arrive early for parking and a good spot. June and September are quieter and cooler: good for walking and photos, though some beach bars and lifeguard posts may not be open yet or may already have closed for the year.

Who Playa de Carnota suits

Sand makes it comfortable for sunbathing, building sandcastles and long walks along the water line. Easy access makes it practical for families, older visitors and anyone with a pushchair. It is flagged as a hidden gem, so it suits travellers who prefer quieter places over the headline beaches. Food and drink close by make it easy to stay for the whole day.
