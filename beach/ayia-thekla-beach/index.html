Ayia Thekla Beach lies on the coast west of Ayia Napa beside a small seaside chapel. The sand is soft, the water is calm and shallow, and the beach is quieter than the main resort beaches, which makes it popular with local families.

Planning your visit to Ayia Thekla Beach

Sand beaches are usually gentle underfoot and easy for swimmers and children, though they can be exposed to wind and waves, so check conditions on the day. Bring a hat and extra water, as shade can be scarce away from facilities.

Access is straightforward, with an easy approach that suits most people, including those with children or limited mobility.

The usual season runs May–October. Visiting near the start or end of that window usually means fewer people, but the sea is cooler and some facilities may not be open yet or may have closed for the year. We flag this beach as a hidden gem, so it is quieter than the headline beaches nearby, but it is no secret: arrive early in peak summer.

Best months to visit Ayia Thekla Beach

The swimming season here runs from May to October. June, July, August, September are the warmest and busiest, with the sea around 26 °C, so arrive early for parking and a good spot. May and October are quieter and cooler: good for walking and photos, though some beach bars and lifeguard posts may not be open yet or may already have closed for the year.

Who Ayia Thekla Beach suits

The soft sand is good for lying out, playing with children and strolling along the shore. With easy access it works well for families, older visitors and people with pushchairs. It is flagged as a hidden gem, so it suits travellers who prefer quieter places over the headline beaches. Food and drink close by make it easy to stay for the whole day.

Getting there and nearby

Free parking near the chapel and the beach. Use the map link above for directions. If you are planning a day around it, the closest other beaches on our list are Makronissos Beach, Ayia Napa (2 km), Landa Beach, Ayia Napa (3 km), Nissi Beach, Ayia Napa (3 km).
