Playa de las Catedrales

A Galician coast of towering cliffs carved by the Atlantic into giant arches and caves, walkable only when the tide goes out. It is one of Spain most photographed natural monuments.

Planning your visit to Playa de las Catedrales

Rocky coves often have clear, deeper water that suits snorkelling, but the entry can be slippery. Wear water shoes, enter slowly and avoid swimming when there is a swell.

Access is rated moderate: expect stairs, a short walk or a rough road, and wear proper shoes rather than flip-flops.

The usual season runs June–September. Visiting near the start or end of that window usually means fewer people, but the sea is cooler and some facilities may not be open yet or may have closed for the year.

Best months to visit Playa de las Catedrales

The swimming season here runs from June to September. July, August are the warmest and busiest, with the sea around 18 °C, so arrive early for parking and a good spot. June and September are quieter and cooler: good for walking and photos, though some beach bars and lifeguard posts may not be open yet or may already have closed for the year.

Who Playa de las Catedrales suits

A rocky shore suits confident swimmers and snorkellers more than families with small children; bring water shoes. Expect a short walk or uneven ground, so it suits most visitors with sensible footwear. Food and drink close by make it easy to stay for the whole day.

Getting there and nearby

Two large free car parks, one asphalted and one gravel; they fill quickly. The beach sits at about 43.554, -7.157 on the map, so use the map link above for directions.
