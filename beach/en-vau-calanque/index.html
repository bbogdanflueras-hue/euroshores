Planning your visit to En-Vau Calanque

Pebble beaches usually mean clear water but awkward walking. Bring water shoes and a thick towel or mat to sit on.

Access is rated challenging and involves a steep hike. Take plenty of water, start early and allow enough daylight for the walk back.

The usual season runs May–September. Visiting near the start or end of that window usually means fewer people, but the sea is cooler and some facilities may not be open yet or may have closed for the year. We flag this beach as a hidden gem, so it is quieter than the headline beaches nearby, but it is no secret: arrive early in peak summer.

Best months to visit En-Vau Calanque

The swimming season here runs from May to September. June, July, August are the warmest and busiest, with the sea around 22 °C, so arrive early for parking and a good spot. May and September are quieter and cooler: good for walking and photos, though some beach bars and lifeguard posts may not be open yet or may already have closed for the year.

Who En-Vau Calanque suits

Pebbles and clear water suit swimmers and snorkellers; bring water shoes for an easier entry. Access is demanding, so it suits fit, well-prepared visitors; wear sturdy shoes and carry water. It is flagged as a hidden gem, so it suits travellers who prefer quieter places over the headline beaches. There are few or no facilities on the sand, so bring water, food and shade.

Getting there and nearby

No road access: reach it on foot by marked trails from the Col de la Gardiole or from Cassis along the GR 51-98, or by boat or kayak. The beach sits at about 43.200, 5.502 on the map, so use the map link above for directions. If you are planning a day around it, the closest other beaches on our list are Calanque de Port-Pin (1 km), Plage de la Grande Mer, Cassis (3 km), Calanque de Sugiton (4 km).
