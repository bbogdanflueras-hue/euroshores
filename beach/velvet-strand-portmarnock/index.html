Velvet Strand, Portmarnock

The Velvet Strand is a long beach of firm, smooth sand north of Dublin, with views to Ireland's Eye and Lambay Island. In 1930 Charles Kingsford Smith took off from here on a transatlantic flight.

Planning your visit to Velvet Strand, Portmarnock

Sand beaches are usually gentle underfoot and easy for swimmers and children, though they can be exposed to wind and waves, so check conditions on the day. Bring a hat and extra water, as shade can be scarce away from facilities.

Access is rated easy, so most visitors can reach the beach without a long walk or special equipment.

The usual season runs June–August. Visiting near the start or end of that window usually means fewer people, but the sea is cooler and some facilities may not be open yet or may have closed for the year.

Best months to visit Velvet Strand, Portmarnock

Velvet Strand, Portmarnock is at its best from June to August. Expect the sea near 15 °C and the biggest crowds in July, so get there early for parking. A visit in June or August is calmer, but check that facilities are open.

Who Velvet Strand, Portmarnock suits

This is a sand beach, which makes it easy for sunbathing, sandcastles and a long walk at the water edge. The simple approach makes it a practical choice for families and visitors who prefer not to walk far. Food and drink close by make it easy to stay for the whole day.

Getting there and nearby

Parking along the coast road; DART trains to Portmarnock. Use the map link above for directions. If you are planning a day around it, the closest other beaches on our list are Curracloe Beach (117 km), Rossnowlagh Beach (185 km), Lahinch Beach (222 km).
