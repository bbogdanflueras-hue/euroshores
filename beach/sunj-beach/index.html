Planning your visit to Sunj Beach

Sand beaches are usually gentle underfoot and easy for swimmers and children, though they can be exposed to wind and waves, so check conditions on the day. Bring a hat and extra water, as shade can be scarce away from facilities.

Access is rated moderate: expect stairs, a short walk or a rough road, and wear proper shoes rather than flip-flops.

The usual season runs June–September. Visiting near the start or end of that window usually means fewer people, but the sea is cooler and some facilities may not be open yet or may have closed for the year.

Best months to visit Sunj Beach

The swimming season here runs from June to September. July, August are the warmest and busiest, with the sea around 24 °C, so arrive early for parking and a good spot. June and September are quieter and cooler: good for walking and photos, though some beach bars and lifeguard posts may not be open yet or may already have closed for the year.

Who Sunj Beach suits

Sand makes it comfortable for sunbathing, building sandcastles and long walks along the water line. Expect a short walk or uneven ground, so it suits most visitors with sensible footwear. Food and drink close by make it easy to stay for the whole day.

Getting there and nearby

There are no cars on the island. Take the Elaphiti ferry from Gruz harbour in Dubrovnik (about 45 minutes), then walk 25 to 30 minutes through the forest or take a golf cart taxi in season. The beach sits at about 42.680, 17.952 on the map, so use the map link above for directions. If you are planning a day around it, the closest other beaches on our list are Donje Celo Beach, Kolocep (4 km), Sjekirica Beach, Brsecine (5 km), Copacabana Beach, Dubrovnik (10 km).
