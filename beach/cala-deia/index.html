Cala Deia

4.5 / 5 Small pebble cove under the Tramuntana mountains in Deia, Mallorca · Hidden gem

A tiny pebble and rock cove at the end of a narrow valley below the village of Deia, with very clear water and a fishing-hut feel. It is popular, so it fills up quickly in summer.

Planning your visit to Cala Deia

Rocky coves often have clear, deeper water that suits snorkelling, but the entry can be slippery. Wear water shoes, enter slowly and avoid swimming when there is a swell.

Access is rated moderate: expect stairs, a short walk or a rough road, and wear proper shoes rather than flip-flops.

The usual season runs May–October. Visiting near the start or end of that window usually means fewer people, but the sea is cooler and some facilities may not be open yet or may have closed for the year. We flag this beach as a hidden gem, so it is quieter than the headline beaches nearby, but it is no secret: arrive early in peak summer.

Best months to visit Cala Deia

The swimming season here runs from May to October. June, July, August, September are the warmest and busiest, with the sea around 24 °C, so arrive early for parking and a good spot. May and October are quieter and cooler: good for walking and photos, though some beach bars and lifeguard posts may not be open yet or may already have closed for the year.

Who Cala Deia suits

A rocky shore suits confident swimmers and snorkellers more than families with small children; bring water shoes. Expect a short walk or uneven ground, so it suits most visitors with sensible footwear. It is flagged as a hidden gem, so it suits travellers who prefer quieter places over the headline beaches. Food and drink close by make it easy to stay for the whole day.

Getting there and nearby

Very limited parking on the winding road; plans for a bigger car park have been announced. The beach sits at about 39.749, 2.647 on the map, so use the map link above for directions.
