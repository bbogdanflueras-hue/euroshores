Plage Notre-Dame, Porquerolles

4.7 / 5 Pine-fringed bay of white sand on a car-free island in Porquerolles Island, Var, Provence · Hidden gem

Photo: PQR, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Save on Pinterest

Plage Notre-Dame is a long crescent of white sand on the south-east of Porquerolles, a car-free island off the Var coast, with pine and eucalyptus trees behind and green-turquoise water. The island is a national park, so the sea is clean and clear.

Practical info for Plage Notre-Dame, Porquerolles

AccessFerry from La Tour Fondue (Giens) in about 20 minutes, every 30 to 45 minutes in summer; a few crossings leave from Hyeres. There are no cars on Porquerolles, so rent a bike at the port or walk.
Car parkingNo cars on the island. On the mainland the Tour Fondue car park fills in July and August and is capped at about EUR 21 a day; a free car park near Hyeres port has a summer shuttle to the ferry.
Food & drinkNo shops at the beach, so buy supplies before you cross. Cafes and restaurants are in the village by the port, and the nearby Plage d Argent has a restaurant.
Good to knowThe most popular and busiest beach on the island: avoid mid to late July and August, and take an early ferry.

Planning your visit to Plage Notre-Dame, Porquerolles

Sand beaches are usually gentle underfoot and easy for swimmers and children, though they can be exposed to wind and waves, so check conditions on the day. Bring a hat and extra water, as shade can be scarce away from facilities.

Access is rated easy, so most visitors can reach the beach without a long walk or special equipment.

The usual season runs June–September. Visiting near the start or end of that window usually means fewer people, but the sea is cooler and some facilities may not be open yet or may have closed for the year. We flag this beach as a hidden gem, so it is quieter than the headline beaches nearby, but it is no secret: arrive early in peak summer.

Best months to visit Plage Notre-Dame, Porquerolles

The swimming season here runs from June to September. July, August are the warmest and busiest, with the sea around 24 °C, so arrive early for parking and a good spot. June and September are quieter and cooler: good for walking and photos, though some beach bars and lifeguard posts may not be open yet or may already have closed for the year.

Who Plage Notre-Dame, Porquerolles suits

Sand makes it comfortable for sunbathing, building sandcastles and long walks along the water line. Easy access makes it practical for families, older visitors and anyone with a pushchair. It is flagged as a hidden gem, so it suits travellers who prefer quieter places over the headline beaches. There are few or no facilities on the sand, so bring water, food and shade.

Getting there and nearby

No cars on the island; park at Tour Fondue (Giens) or Hyeres and take the ferry. The beach sits at about 42.993, 6.230 on the map, so use the map link above for directions. If you are planning a day around it, the closest other beaches on our list are Plage d'Argent (1 km), Plage Notre-Dame (2 km), La Courtade Beach (2 km).
