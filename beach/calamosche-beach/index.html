Planning your visit to Calamosche Beach

Sand beaches are usually gentle underfoot and easy for swimmers and children, though they can be exposed to wind and waves, so check conditions on the day. Bring a hat and extra water, as shade can be scarce away from facilities.

Access is rated moderate: expect stairs, a short walk or a rough road, and wear proper shoes rather than flip-flops.

The usual season runs May–October. Visiting near the start or end of that window usually means fewer people, but the sea is cooler and some facilities may not be open yet or may have closed for the year. We flag this beach as a hidden gem, so it is quieter than the headline beaches nearby, but it is no secret: arrive early in peak summer.

Best months to visit Calamosche Beach

The swimming season here runs from May to October. June, July, August, September are the warmest and busiest, with the sea around 24 °C, so arrive early for parking and a good spot. May and October are quieter and cooler: good for walking and photos, though some beach bars and lifeguard posts may not be open yet or may already have closed for the year.

Who Calamosche Beach suits

Sand makes it comfortable for sunbathing, building sandcastles and long walks along the water line. Expect a short walk or uneven ground, so it suits most visitors with sensible footwear. It is flagged as a hidden gem, so it suits travellers who prefer quieter places over the headline beaches. Food and drink close by make it easy to stay for the whole day.

Getting there and nearby

Private car park at the reserve, about 5 EUR reported, plus a reserve entry fee. The beach sits at about 36.824, 15.106 on the map, so use the map link above for directions.
