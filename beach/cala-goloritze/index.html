Planning your visit to Cala Goloritzé

Pebble beaches usually mean clear water but awkward walking. Bring water shoes and a thick towel or mat to sit on.

Access is rated challenging and involves a steep hike. Take plenty of water, start early and allow enough daylight for the walk back.

The usual season runs June–September. Visiting near the start or end of that window usually means fewer people, but the sea is cooler and some facilities may not be open yet or may have closed for the year. We flag this beach as a hidden gem, so it is quieter than the headline beaches nearby, but it is no secret: arrive early in peak summer.

Best months to visit Cala Goloritzé

The swimming season here runs from June to September. July, August are the warmest and busiest, with the sea around 24 °C, so arrive early for parking and a good spot. June and September are quieter and cooler: good for walking and photos, though some beach bars and lifeguard posts may not be open yet or may already have closed for the year.

Who Cala Goloritzé suits

Pebbles and clear water suit swimmers and snorkellers; bring water shoes for an easier entry. Access is demanding, so it suits fit, well-prepared visitors; wear sturdy shoes and carry water. It is flagged as a hidden gem, so it suits travellers who prefer quieter places over the headline beaches. There are few or no facilities on the sand, so bring water, food and shade.

Getting there and nearby

Su Porteddu car park (included in the ticket), then a steep hike. The beach sits at about 40.117, 9.700 on the map, so use the map link above for directions. If you are planning a day around it, the closest other beaches on our list are Cala Mariolu (2 km), Cala Sisine (9 km), Cala Luna (14 km).
