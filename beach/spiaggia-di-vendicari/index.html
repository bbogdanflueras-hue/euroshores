Vendicari is a nature reserve on the south-east coast of Sicily, with several long beaches of pale sand, salt marshes that attract flamingos in spring and autumn, and an old tuna fishery. Facilities are minimal, which keeps the coast wild.

Planning your visit to Spiaggia di Vendicari

Sand beaches are usually gentle underfoot and easy for swimmers and children, though they can be exposed to wind and waves, so check conditions on the day. Bring a hat and extra water, as shade can be scarce away from facilities.

Access is rated moderate: expect stairs, a short walk or a rough road, and wear proper shoes rather than flip-flops.

The usual season runs June–September. Visiting near the start or end of that window usually means fewer people, but the sea is cooler and some facilities may not be open yet or may have closed for the year. We flag this beach as a hidden gem, so it is quieter than the headline beaches nearby, but it is no secret: arrive early in peak summer.

Best months to visit Spiaggia di Vendicari

The swimming season here runs from June to September. July, August are the warmest and busiest, with the sea around 26 °C, so arrive early for parking and a good spot. June and September are quieter and cooler: good for walking and photos, though some beach bars and lifeguard posts may not be open yet or may already have closed for the year.

Who Spiaggia di Vendicari suits

Sand makes it comfortable for sunbathing, building sandcastles and long walks along the water line. Expect a short walk or uneven ground, so it suits most visitors with sensible footwear. It is flagged as a hidden gem, so it suits travellers who prefer quieter places over the headline beaches. There are few or no facilities on the sand, so bring water, food and shade.

Getting there and nearby
