Budget Beach Holidays in Europe

2 min read · Last updated 2026-10-26

Beaches themselves are free almost everywhere in Europe; costs come from sunbeds, parking, boat access and food. With a little planning you can enjoy some of the continent's best shores on a modest budget. This guide covers where the money really goes and how to keep it in your pocket.

Where the money goes

  • Sunbed and umbrella hire: often the biggest daily cost at developed beaches such as Spiaggia Grande or Ghadira Bay, and priced highest in July and August.
  • Parking: paid, and sometimes limited, at beaches such as Zlatni Rat and Palombaggia.
  • Boats and tickets: some beaches need a boat or a booking, for example Blue Lagoon and Balos Lagoon.
  • Food and drink: beach bars charge more than village shops, and prices drop noticeably a few streets inland.

Cheap and free beaches worth the trip

  • Es Trenc in Mallorca has long stretches with no sunbeds, so you can bring your own towel and shade.
  • Banje Beach in Dubrovnik is free to enter, with a paid beach club alongside.
  • Bacvice in Split is a city beach reachable on foot, so you skip car costs entirely.
  • Ksamil Beaches, Dhermi and Drymades in Albania are among Europe's lower-cost coastal destinations, with free areas next to the paid beach clubs.
  • Praia da Marinha and Praia Dona Ana in the Algarve need only a car or a short walk.

Money-saving habits

  • Travel in shoulder season: late May, June and September have lower flights and accommodation than July and August.
  • Bring your own umbrella and snacks instead of renting sunbeds and buying lunch on the sand.
  • Stay in the next village rather than right at the beach, then walk, cycle or take public transport in.
  • Choose self-catering, so a supermarket shop replaces some restaurant meals.
  • Carry cash. Some small beach kiosks, parking attendants and boat operators do not take cards.
  • Arrive early. Free parking spots go first and early arrivals often avoid sunbed fees altogether.

Where to save, and where not to

Do not skimp on safety. Avoid unofficial boat operators without life jackets, and do not leave valuables unattended. Spend a little more on sturdy water shoes if a beach is pebbly or rocky, such as Stiniva or Gjipe. It is better to pay for a licensed boat to a remote beach than to risk an unsafe path.

Sample budget idea

A week split between a village base and day trips by bus, with a picnic on most days and one beach-club day as a treat, costs far less than a week of resort sunbeds. Prices change from season to season and by country, so check current rates before you book.
