Best Snorkelling & Diving Spots

2 min read · Last updated 2026-10-16

Clear water, rocky shelves and sheltered coves make some beaches excellent for snorkelling and shore diving. This guide lists the spots on our list where visibility is typically good, along with what to check before you get in the water. Marine life varies by season and by site, and conditions can change from day to day.

Best snorkelling spots on our list

  • Blue Lagoon at Comino: shallow, clear water over pale sand and rock; very busy in peak season, so go early.
  • St Peter's Pool in Malta: a natural rock pool with clear water, popular with swimmers and snorkellers. It has no facilities, so bring water and shoes.
  • Ksamil Beaches in Albania: small islets and clear shallows close to shore.
  • Stiniva on Vis: a sheltered cove with clear water beneath steep cliffs.
  • Cala Mariolu and Cala Goloritze in Sardinia: very clear water along rocky shores, usually reached by boat or by a long walk.
  • Saleccia in Corsica: a wide bay with clear turquoise water.
  • Sarakiniko on Milos: white volcanic rock and clear blue water, good for swimming and snorkelling from the rock ledges.
  • Potami Beach near Himara: clear water fed by freshwater springs.

Sheltered bays for beginners

  • Ses Illetes in Formentera: shallow and calm on windless days.
  • Cala Macarelleta in Menorca: a small, sheltered cove with clear water.
  • Ghajn Tuffieha Bay and Ramla Bay in Malta: sandy bays where the water is clear in calm weather, although waves can be rough in strong wind.

Atlantic coves

Praia da Marinha and Praia Dona Ana in the Algarve have rock formations and clear water on calm days, but the Atlantic is cooler and swell can arrive quickly. A thicker wetsuit top helps in June and September.

What to bring

  • Mask, snorkel and fins, or hire them locally in season.
  • A rash vest or thin wetsuit for sun and for cooler water.
  • Water shoes for rocky entries and sea urchins.
  • A surface marker buoy if you swim away from the shore.
  • Reef-safe sun protection and plenty of drinking water.

Safety and the sea

  • Check wind, swell and currents before you go in, and never snorkel alone.
  • Stay away from boat lanes, and keep to marked swimming areas where they exist.
  • Watch for sea urchins and jellyfish, and do not touch marine life.
  • Do not take shells, corals or other natural items, and avoid trampling seagrass.
  • Scuba diving needs a certified operator, so ask local dive centres about courses, guided dives and rules.

When to go

Water clarity is usually good in late spring and early summer, while sea temperature peaks in late summer. Weekday mornings are best for calm water and fewer people in the bay.
