Best Time to Visit Each Country

3 min read · Last updated 2026-10-19

Mediterranean beaches are at their best from late May to mid-June and again in September: warm sea, long days and noticeably fewer people than in July and August. Sea temperature lags the air, so water is usually warmest in late summer, while the first half of June can still feel fresh to swim in. Every beach page on EuroShores lists a typical in-season water temperature and a recommended window, and this guide pulls those together by country.

Country by country

  • Greece: May–October on the islands, with the warmest sea in August and September. Beaches such as Elafonissi Beach, Balos Lagoon and Porto Katsiki are best from late May to June or in September, when the boats and car parks are calmer. The Aegean meltemi wind is strongest in July and August and can make exposed beaches choppy, so check the forecast for the side of the island you are on.
  • Italy: June–September for swimming, May and October for walking and quiet. Sardinia's famous coves such as Cala Mariolu and La Pelosa have visitor limits or booking rules in peak season, so plan ahead for July and August. Amalfi-coast style beaches like Spiaggia Grande in Positano are far pleasanter in May, June and September.
  • Spain: May–October on the Balearic Islands, where Es Trenc, Ses Illetes and Cala Macarelleta are lovely in June and September. The Atlantic north is different: As Catedrais in Galicia is best from May to September, and the tide matters more than the month.
  • Portugal (Algarve): June–September for the beach, with May and October often sunny too. The Atlantic stays cooler than the Mediterranean all year, so water that feels bracing in June is milder by September. Praia da Marinha, Benagil Cove and Praia Dona Ana are best on calm-sea days.
  • Croatia: June–September for swimming. Zlatni Rat, Punta Rata and Banje Beach are busy in July and August, so go early or choose a shoulder month.
  • Montenegro: June–September. Sveti Stefan and Jaz Beach are at their liveliest in July and August, with festivals and concerts at Jaz.
  • Albania: June–September. The Riviera at Dhermi, Jale and Ksamil Beaches fills in August, while June and September bring warm water and easier parking.
  • Malta and Gozo: June–September for the beach, with May and October still good for Blue Lagoon and St Peter's Pool. Sandy bays such as Golden Bay and Ramla Bay are crowded on summer weekends.
  • Corsica (France): June–September. Palombaggia, Santa Giulia and Saleccia are best in June or September, when the sea is warm and the roads are quieter.

Why shoulder season works

In July and August the most famous beaches fill by late morning, parking is the first thing to go, and boat-only or booking-limited beaches can sell out days ahead. Late May, June and September give you most of the warmth with far more space, and accommodation is usually cheaper. The trade-off is that some beach bars, boat trips and shuttle services run reduced timetables in the first and last weeks of the season.

Picking the right month for your priorities

  • Warmest water: late August to mid-September in most of the Mediterranean.
  • Fewest people: the last week of May, the first half of June and the second half of September.
  • Best light for photos: early morning in June or September, before crowds and haze build up.
  • Lowest prices: the shoulder months, and midweek stays rather than weekends.

Practical tips

Check live conditions on the day: wind, swell, jellyfish alerts and any access restrictions. Some beaches have temporary closures or booking systems that change from year to year, and each beach page notes when its information was last checked. If your dates are fixed in July or August, pick the most remote beaches on our list and arrive before 9 AM.
