Canary Islands Beaches in Winter: Which Island and Which Coast

3 min read · Last updated 2026-11-25

The Canary Islands are the warmest place in Europe from November to March. The sea stays around 19 to 20 C through the winter and daytime highs are usually around 20 C or a little more. But the islands are not all the same, and where you stay on each island matters as much as which island you choose.

The simple rule: go south

The trade winds blow from the north-east for much of the year. They bring cloud and showers to the north coasts, which are greener and cooler, while the south and south-west coasts are drier, sunnier and more sheltered. For a beach holiday in winter, stay in the south. Visit the north on a day trip for scenery.

Sea temperatures in winter

  • January: Tenerife about 20 C; Gran Canaria, Lanzarote and Fuerteventura about 19 C.
  • February: Tenerife and Gran Canaria about 19 C; Lanzarote and Fuerteventura about 18 C.
  • March: the coolest sea of the year in most of the islands, at around 18 to 19 C, though daytime highs are back near 20 C.

Tenerife: the warmest sea and the most choice

  • Playa de Los Cristianos: a sheltered town beach in the sunny south, easy for families.
  • Playa de El Medano: a long natural beach in the south-east. It is often windy, which is why windsurfers love it.
  • Playa de las Teresitas: a calm golden beach near Santa Cruz, protected by a breakwater. It is on the north-east side, so it can be cloudier.
  • Playa de Benijo: a wild black-sand beach in the Anaga mountains. Beautiful for a walk, but the sea is often rough in winter.

Mount Teide can have snow in winter, so you can see snow and sit on the beach on the same day.

Gran Canaria: dunes, resorts and a city beach

  • Maspalomas Dunes and Playa del Ingles: the big sandy beaches of the sunny south coast.
  • Playa de Amadores: a sheltered bay with calm water, good for children.
  • Playa de las Canteras: the city beach of Las Palmas, with a natural reef that keeps the water calm. The north of the island has more cloud in winter.

Lanzarote: volcanic scenery and sheltered coves

  • Playa de Papagayo: sheltered coves of golden sand at the southern tip, reached by a dirt road.
  • Playa de Famara: a long surf beach below cliffs in the north-west. Windy and with strong currents, better for walks and surfing than swimming.

Fuerteventura: the longest beaches, and the most wind

  • Playa de Sotavento and Costa Calma: long white beaches and a tidal lagoon on the Jandia peninsula.
  • El Cotillo: calm lagoons in the north-west, good on days when the wind is light.
  • Playa de Cofete: a remote, empty beach under the mountains, reached by a rough track. Swimming is dangerous here because of strong currents.

Fuerteventura is the windiest of the islands, which makes it a top spot for kitesurfing and windsurfing but less comfortable for lying on the sand.

Which island should you choose?

  • Warmest sea and most variety: Tenerife (south).
  • Sunny resort beaches and a lively city: Gran Canaria.
  • Scenery and quieter coves: Lanzarote.
  • Huge empty beaches and water sports: Fuerteventura.
