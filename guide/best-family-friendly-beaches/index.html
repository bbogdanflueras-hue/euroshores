Best Family-Friendly Beaches

2 min read · Last updated 2026-10-19

The best beaches for children combine gentle entry, shallow water, easy access and some facilities. This guide shortlists the easiest options on our list and explains what to check before you go. Every beach page includes an access rating and a note on facilities, so you can compare quickly.

What makes a beach family friendly

  • Shallow, gradual entry: toddlers and weaker swimmers can paddle safely.
  • Easy access: a short flat walk, with no long staircase or steep path that is hard with a pushchair.
  • Facilities: toilets, showers, shade and somewhere to buy water or food.
  • Sand rather than sharp pebbles: though water shoes help on pebble beaches.
  • Lifeguards: especially in July and August, though coverage varies by beach and by season.

Our top picks for families

  • Rajska Plaza on Rab: a long sandy beach where the shallow water reaches far out, with bars, showers and lifeguards.
  • Ramla Bay and Ghadira Bay in Malta: wide sandy bays with gentle entry and nearby facilities.
  • Es Trenc in Mallorca: soft sand and shallow water, with a few simple beach bars.
  • Ses Illetes in Formentera: very shallow turquoise water, although it is busy in summer.
  • La Pelosa in Sardinia: shallow, calm water. Check the current access rules, because visitor numbers are managed in peak season.
  • Palombaggia and Santa Giulia in Corsica: fine sand and calm bays with facilities nearby.
  • Bacvice in Split: a city beach with shallow, sandy-bottomed water and plenty of nearby cafes.
  • Golden Bay in Malta: a wide sandy bay with facilities and a lifeguard in season.

Beaches to skip with very young children

Some of the most photogenic beaches are poor choices for small children. Cala Goloritze, Stiniva and Gjipe require a challenging walk or a boat. Navagio Beach is currently closed to landings. Praia da Marinha has steep access and rocky ground. Check each beach page for its access rating before you plan.

Safety checklist

  • Check wind and wave conditions on the day, and look for flag systems where they exist.
  • Use sun protection and shade, and bring more water than you think you need.
  • Keep children within arm's reach near the water, even in shallow bays.
  • Bring water shoes for pebble beaches and rocky entries.
  • Arrive early in July and August, because parking and shade fill up fast.

Best months with children

June and September are often ideal: warm enough to swim, with fewer crowds and lower prices. In the peak weeks of July and August, plan around the heat by going to the beach early morning or late afternoon.
