How to Pick the Right Beach in Europe: Sand, Pebbles or Rock

1 min read · Last updated 2026-11-06

Europe's beaches are not all the same kind of beach, and choosing the wrong type is the most common way to spoil a day. This guide explains the three main surfaces, what to check before you go, and which type suits which trip.

Sand, pebbles and rock

  • Sand: gentle entry, easy walking barefoot and the best choice for small children. It can get hot at midday and is often the busiest type.
  • Pebbles and shingle: usually clearer water, but the bottom drops quickly and stones are hard on tender feet. Water shoes help a lot.
  • Rock and cove: the best snorkelling and the quietest spots, but they need shoes, some confidence in the water and often a walk to get there.

Check three things before you go

The bottom. Sand slopes gently, pebbles drop fast and rock can hide sea urchins. Exposure. An open bay facing the wind can be rough one day and glassy the next, so check the forecast the evening before. Access. Some of the best coves need a steep path or a boat, and the walk back up is harder in the afternoon heat.

Match the beach to your trip

  • Small children: sand, shallow entry, easy access and a kiosk nearby.
  • Snorkelling: rocky or pebble coves in calm weather.
  • A quiet day: beaches that need a walk, visited early in the morning.
  • Mixed groups: a long beach with both a lively end and a quiet end.
