Beach Parking in Europe: Free or Paid? Data From 852 Beaches

4 min read · Last updated 2026-11-25

Just over half of Europe's best-known beaches still have free parking nearby. We checked the parking notes for 852 beaches in nine countries on EuroShores: 52% have free parking reported, 37% charge, and 6% cannot be reached by car at all. Where a daily price is reported, the typical charge is about 6 euros a day, but a few famous beaches cost 15 euros or more.

What the numbers say

  • Greece and Portugal are the easiest for free parking. Two thirds of Greek beaches and three in five Portuguese ones have free parking reported, often a simple dirt or roadside lot.
  • Italy is the only big country where paid parking is the norm. More than half of Italian beaches charge, often by the hour in towns, and some popular beaches use booking systems with limited places.
  • Montenegro and Albania charge at about two in three beaches, usually small fees collected by local lots or beach bars.
  • Islands and protected areas are where cars are banned or impractical. 51 beaches are reached only on foot or by boat, or have a car-free access zone in summer.

The most expensive beach parking we found

These are the highest daily charges reported for public or commercial lots at beaches on EuroShores. Prices are approximate and change by season.

  • Zlatni Rat, Croatia: about 10 to 25 euros a day, depending on the lot; cheaper lots a few hundred metres away.
  • Psarou, Mykonos: about 15 to 20 euros, and the lot fills fast in July and August.
  • Alassio, Italian Riviera: about 15 euros a day reported across the road from the beach.
  • Scivu, Sardinia: about 15 euros for the day in a gravel lot of about 70 spaces.
  • Sant'Andrea, Elba: about 14 euros a day at the closest private lot; a small free car park is about 200 m away.
  • Kamenjak, Croatia: a car day pass of about 10 to 15 euros for the nature park; a free lot sits before the gate.
  • Cabasson, French Riviera: about 11 to 13 euros in the main car park.
  • Porto Katsiki, Lefkada: about 10 euros a day for a car.

How to pay less

  • Arrive before 10 am in July and August. Many free lots are full by mid-morning, which pushes late arrivals into paid lots.
  • Look one street back. At several of the beaches above, cheaper or free parking is a few hundred metres away.
  • Use the bus or boat where it exists. Beaches such as Mondello and Portinho da Arrabida have summer car restrictions or a direct bus that avoids the parking problem altogether.
  • Carry coins and a little cash. Small lots in Greece, Albania and Montenegro are often run by a person with a cash box, and some machines do not take cards.

How we collected the data

Each beach page on EuroShores has a practical info section compiled from official tourism pages, local guides and recent visitor reports, mostly from 2025 and 2026. For this study we read the parking note for every beach that has one (852 of them) and classed it as free, paid, no car access, or mixed. Prices come only from notes that give a figure; daily fees were found for about 50 beaches, so treat the averages as a guide, not a survey. Parking rules change every season, so always check signs on arrival.
